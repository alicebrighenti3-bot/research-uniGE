# Article architecture (FASE 3)

## Proposed title
**Multiparametric Ultrasound of the Salivary Glands: From Normal Sonoanatomy to
Integrated Diagnostic Pathways for Inflammatory, Autoimmune, and Neoplastic Disease**

## Running title
Multiparametric US of the Salivary Glands

## Article type
Narrative / state-of-the-art review with critical evidence appraisal (target category:
Ultrasound in Medicine & Biology, European Radiology, Insights into Imaging, or
Ultraschall in der Medizin — final target journal choice affects only formatting/length,
not content)

## Central question (stated explicitly in Introduction and revisited in Discussion)
How can conventional and advanced multiparametric ultrasound be integrated to improve
diagnosis, characterization, tissue sampling and management of normal and pathological
salivary glands, and which components of this approach are sufficiently validated for
current clinical practice?

## Key messages (3–5, to appear in Introduction and be echoed in Conclusion)
1. B-mode + Doppler US remains the validated first-line technique for salivary gland
   disease, especially sialolithiasis and initial tumor triage — but grayscale signs of
   malignancy overlap substantially with benign appearance and must not be
   over-interpreted.
2. Advanced techniques (SMI/MVI, elastography, CEUS) add genuine diagnostic information
   but none currently supports a universal, vendor-independent cut-off; the strongest
   validated advanced application is OMERACT grey-scale scoring for Sjögren's syndrome.
3. Core needle biopsy has consistently better sensitivity for malignancy than FNA across
   independent comparative studies and should be considered earlier in the pathway than
   current practice in many centers reflects — while the Milan cytopathology system
   remains the reporting backbone for FNA.
4. A genuinely multiparametric, algorithmically fused interpretation (as opposed to
   clinician-level qualitative multi-technique reading) is not yet a validated
   technology — this is a proposal for structured practice, not a summary of proven
   models.
5. AI/radiomics and US–MRI fusion for salivary glands are real but early-stage research
   directions, not yet clinically deployed tools.

## Differentiation vs closest existing reviews (see gap analysis for full detail)
Explicitly cited and differentiated from Johnson et al. 2025 (*Ultraschall Med*, CME,
scope to be confirmed by author before submission) and Martino et al. 2020 (*Gland
Surgery*, parotid-only, pre-AI). Differentiators: all major glands, explicit evidence-
grading with discordance analysis, integrated interventional-US section, currency
through 2026, and an explicitly labeled author-proposed diagnostic algorithm.

Each technique section (4–7) follows the same internal pattern: principle → technique/
optimization → normal findings → evidence in tumor characterization → evidence in
non-neoplastic disease → critical appraisal (validated / promising / experimental) →
comparison with the other techniques.

## Figures (5) — content specification

**Figure 1. Normal sonoanatomy of parotid, submandibular and sublingual glands.**
Panels: (A) parotid transverse scan with retromandibular vein/facial nerve plane
landmark; (B) parotid longitudinal scan showing superficial/deep lobe relationship to
mandibular ramus; (C) submandibular gland with facial artery/vein and mylohyoid muscle;
(D) sublingual gland floor-of-mouth scan; (E) schematic diagram of duct anatomy
(Stensen's, Wharton's) with expected non-visualization on normal US. Message: establish
the normal anatomic and Doppler-landmark reference that every later pathology figure is
read against.

**Figure 2. Multiparametric ultrasound workflow (schematic).**
Flow diagram: clinical question → B-mode → Doppler → MVI → elastography → CEUS
(selected cases) → tissue sampling → CT/MRI (selected cases) → management. Each arrow
annotated with the evidence grade (validated/promising/experimental) of that step, tied
directly to Table 2/8. Message: this is an explicitly author-proposed pathway, not a
validated guideline — labeled as such in the legend.

**Figure 3. Representative multiparametric assessment of a focal salivary gland lesion.**
Panels: (A) B-mode; (B) Color/Power Doppler; (C) MVI/SMI; (D) elastography (SWE map);
(E) CEUS (arterial-phase and time-intensity curve). Message: shows concretely what each
technique adds (or fails to add) to the same lesion, illustrating incremental value and
residual ambiguity rather than a clean diagnosis.

**Figure 4. Diagnostic pathway for focal salivary gland lesions.**
Decision-tree style figure distinguishing when US alone suffices, when advanced US
techniques should be added, when tissue sampling is indicated, and when MRI/CT is
required regardless of US findings (e.g., deep lobe extension, suspected perineural
spread, staging). Message: operationalizes the workflow of Figure 2 into concrete
triage decisions.

**Figure 5. US-guided FNA/CNB procedural approach.**
Panels: (A) needle trajectory diagram avoiding facial nerve plane (retromandibular vein
landmark) and major vessels; (B) B-mode targeting of a parotid lesion with annotated
safe angle of approach; (C) Color Doppler roadmap of vessels to avoid. Message: practical
procedural guidance grounded in the anatomy established in Figure 1.
