## 2. Key diagnostic-accuracy / enhancement-pattern studies (salivary gland CEUS)

1. **Saito et al.**, "Contrast-enhanced ultrasonography for the differential
   diagnosis of pleomorphic adenomas and Warthin tumors in salivary glands."
   *Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology*. 2021. PMID 34195363.
   DOI 10.1002/lio2.565. — **VERIFIED (multi-source: PMC PMC8223473, PubMed,
   Wiley Online Library listing all match title/journal/year).**
   Findings reported: pleomorphic adenoma (PA) showed significantly longer time to
   peak (TTP) and lower maximum wash-in curve gradient than Warthin tumor (WT);
   WT capillary density rate tended to be greater than PA (Sonazoid contrast
   agent, capillary-density-rate analysis method). "Slow-in" pattern more common
   in PA, "slow-out" more common in WT.

2. **Rogalska M, Antkowiak L, Kasperczuk A, Scierski W.** "Contrast-Enhanced
   Ultrasound in the Differentiation between the Most Common Benign Parotid Gland
   Tumors: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Journal of Clinical Medicine*.
   2022;11(24):7360. PMID 36555976 (matched via PubMed listing).
   DOI plausibly 10.3390/jcm11247360 (standard MDPI pattern) — **DOI itself
   UNVERIFIED**, not directly confirmed in a retrieved snippet.
   — **VERIFIED (multi-source: PMC PMC9787854, PubMed, MDPI page, ResearchGate all
   consistent).**
   Methodology: search of PubMed/Embase/Cochrane to 21 July 2022; 15 studies
   included, focused on PA vs WT.
   Reported pooled quantitative findings:
   - Normalized mean transit time (nMTT): PA 1.45 (95% CI 1.31–1.59) vs WT 0.62
     (95% CI 0.51–0.74), p=0.002 (PA significantly higher).
   - Time to peak (TTP): PA 26.92 (95% CI 22.46–31.39) vs WT 16.92 (95% CI
     16.04–17.72), p=0.001 (PA significantly higher).
   - Heterogeneous enhancement texture: significantly more frequent in PA than WT.
   - Enhanced lesion size, enhancement margin, enhancement rim: **not**
     significantly different between PA and WT.
   - Note: this review reports perfusion-parameter meta-analysis, not pooled
     diagnostic sensitivity/specificity for a binary CEUS-based diagnosis —
     **explicit gap**: no pooled accuracy (sens/spec/AUC) figure for CEUS
     distinguishing PA from WT was found in the retrieved summary.

3. **Zhang [authors not fully retrieved]**, "Comparative Study of Qualitative and
   Quantitative Analyses of Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasound and the Diagnostic Value
   of B-Mode and Color Doppler for Common Benign Tumors in the Parotid Gland."
   *Frontiers in Oncology*. 2021;11:669542. DOI 10.3389/fonc.2021.669542. —
   **VERIFIED (multi-source: Frontiers listing + PMC mirror both show identical
   DOI/title)**, but full author list, exact volume/page and journal issue were
   **not** retrieved — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for author names**.
   Reported: cohort of 105 patients with benign parotid tumors. PA mean TTP 17.95 s
   vs WT 10.99 s; mean gradient of wash-in 1.06 (PA) vs 1.90 (WT); both differences
   reported as statistically significant. Three qualitative CEUS enhancement
   patterns described for focal parotid lesions (diffuse homogeneous; heterogeneous;
   no-enhancement/iso-enhancement), with homogeneous and no/iso-enhancement patterns
   favoring benign and heterogeneous pattern raising concern for malignancy.

4. **Wakonig KM, Lerchbaumer MH, Dommerich S, Olze H, Hamm B, Fischer T, Arens P.**
   "Assessment of Parotid Gland Tumors by Means of Quantitative Multiparametric
   Ultrasound (mpUS)." *Diagnostics*. 2023;13(1):12 (published online 2022-12-21).
   DOI 10.3390/diagnostics13010012. — **VERIFIED (multi-source: PMC PMC9818404,
   MDPI page, NCBI listing all consistent).**
   Methodology: 97 parotid gland tumors in 96 patients; standardized protocol
   combining B-mode, shear-wave elastography (SWE), and CEUS.
   Reported finding (SWE arm): SWE differentiated benign subtypes — WT and benign
   lymph nodes softer than PA; the search-summary description of a comparison to
   squamous cell carcinoma stiffness was internally ambiguous in the retrieved
   text (unclear which group was said to have "lower velocities than SCC") —
   **flag: do not cite this specific SCC comparison without full-text
   confirmation.** CEUS-arm-specific quantitative results (AUC, PE, wash-in
   parameters) were **not captured** in the retrieved snippet — **explicit gap**,
   needs full-text retrieval.

5. **Krupa K, Wieczorek P, [name uncertain — rendered "Scrinscaia O" in one search
   summary, likely an OCR/transliteration artifact, re-verify], Puzio A, Kozłowski
   Z, Pietruszewska W, Stefańczyk L.** "Correlations between Contrast-Enhanced
   Ultrasound Imaging and Histopathological Results in Salivary Gland Lesions."
   *Diagnostics*. 2022;12(11):2636. DOI 10.3390/diagnostics12112636. — **VERIFIED
   (multi-source: PMC PMC9689344 x2 listings + MDPI DOI page)** for citation;
   author spelling of the third name is **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** and may be
   inaccurate. Affiliations: Dept. of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Oncology, Dept.
   of Radiology and Diagnostic Imaging, Dept. of Pathology, Medical University of
   Łódź, Poland. Study aim: correlate CEUS imaging with histopathology to assess
   accuracy of CEUS in preoperative differentiation of salivary gland tumors.
   Concluded CEUS is "a promising tool" for preoperative diagnostics. **Explicit
   gap:** specific sensitivity/specificity/accuracy numbers were not retrieved from
   available snippets.

6. **Klotz LV, Ingrisch M, Eichhorn ME, Niemoeller O, Siedek V, Gürkov R, Clevert
   DA.** "Monitoring parotid gland tumors with a new perfusion software for
   contrast-enhanced ultrasound." *Clinical Hemorheology and Microcirculation*.
   2014. DOI 10.3233/CH-141895. PMID 25339101. — **VERIFIED (multi-source: doi.org
   listing snippet, PubMed listing, PMC cross-reference in related-article
   lists).**
   Used VueBox (Bracco, Italy) external perfusion-quantification software on
   CEUS DICOM cine loops; parameters: area under time-intensity curve (AUC), peak
   enhancement (PE), wash-in rate (WiR), wash-in perfusion index (WiPI). Reported:
   AUC and mean transit time (MTT) significantly higher in malignant vs benign
   parotid lesions; AUC, PE, WiR and WiPI significantly differed between malignant
   and benign tumors and between PA and Warthin (cystadenolymphoma).

7. **Klotz LV, Gürkov R, Eichhorn ME, et al.** "Perfusion characteristics of

   Journal of Radiology*. 2013;82:2227–2232. PMID 24029161. — **VERIFIED
   (multi-source: PubMed listing + cross-referenced in several other papers'

8. **[Possible] Küstermeyer J, Klingelhöfer G, Welkoborsky HJ (author string as

   "Perfusionsanalyse innerhalb von Speicheldrüsentumoren bei der

## 4. Comparative studies (CEUS vs Doppler / elastography / MRI)

- **CEUS + strain elastography (SE) study**, PMID 36731495, title "Contrast-
  Enhanced Ultrasound and Strain Elastography for Differentiating Benign and
  Malignant Parotid Tumors." — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE / incomplete
  citation**: only PMID, title, and an AI-summarized finding were retrieved
  (journal, exact year, full author list, and volume/pages were **not**
  confirmed before the search budget for this session was exhausted). Reported
  finding: in a cohort of 115 patients with benign and malignant parotid
  tumors, AUC for predicting malignancy was 0.75 for SE, 0.82 for CEUS, and 0.88
  for combined SE+CEUS. **This citation needs full bibliographic completion
  before use** — do not publish the AUC numbers under this citation without
  independently confirming journal/year via direct PubMed access.

- General qualitative finding (multi-source consistent across several of the
  papers above): malignant parotid tumors more often show blurred/ill-defined
  enhancement margin, larger post-enhancement size, and a "fast-in, fast-out"
  wash-in/wash-out pattern on CEUS, whereas benign lesions (particularly PA)
  tend toward more homogeneous or "slow-in" patterns; overlap with grayscale/
  color Doppler features is noted, especially for PA which can mimic malignancy
  on B-mode/Doppler alone.

- **US vs CT vs MRI meta-analysis (not CEUS-specific)** — a meta-analysis titled
  approximately "Accuracy of diagnosis of salivary gland tumors with the use of
  ultrasonography, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging: a
  meta-analysis" (ScienceDirect PII S2212440314013492) reported pooled
  sensitivity/specificity/AUC of: US 0.629/0.920/0.934; CT 0.830/0.851/0.912;
  MRI 0.807/0.886/0.903. — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** for exact
  journal/year/authors (only the ScienceDirect page identifier was retrieved);
  and importantly **this meta-analysis concerns conventional grayscale
  ultrasound, not CEUS** — usable only as background context on baseline US
  accuracy versus CT/MRI, not as CEUS evidence. Must not be miscited as a CEUS
  comparative study.

- **Direct head-to-head CEUS-vs-MRI comparative studies:** Explicitly **not
  found** in this search. One AI search summary explicitly stated: "direct
  head-to-head comparisons between CEUS and MRI specifically are limited in the
  available literature." — **Confirmed gap.**

- **Multiparametric pathway studies** combining CEUS with B-mode, Doppler, and
  elastography in a single diagnostic algorithm: Wakonig et al. 2023 (Diagnostics,
  item 4 above) and a newer 2026 study — **Wakonig KM et al. (or "Margherita" as
  first-author variant seen in one listing — name inconsistency between two
  citations of what appears to be the same article, re-verify), "Multiparametric
  Ultrasound for Preoperative Assessment of Parotid Tumors: A Novel Diagnostic
  Pathway."** *Head & Neck*. 2026 (early view). PMC12797010.
  DOI 10.1002/hed.70036. — **VERIFIED (multi-source: PMC listing + two Wiley
  Online Library listing variants agree on DOI/journal)**, but the **author-name
  discrepancy ("Wakonig" vs "Margherita" as displayed first author across two
  search snippets) is unresolved** — flag for direct confirmation before citing
  first author. Content/results of this 2026 pathway study were not retrieved in
  detail — **gap**.

## 5. CEUS in malignant / lymphoma / inflammatory salivary gland disease

- Case-based literature analysis (Xu et al., *Quantitative Imaging in Medicine and
  Surgery*, on IgG4-related Mikulicz disease) reported CEUS of the submandibular
  gland: primary Sjögren's syndrome associated with a **low enhancement**
  pattern, whereas IgG4-related Mikulicz disease showed a **"fast-in, fast-out,
  high enhancement"** pattern. — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**; also
  low evidentiary weight regardless of verification status, as this is a case
  report plus literature analysis, not a primary cohort study — should be cited
  (if at all) as hypothesis-generating only.
- No dedicated primary CEUS cohort study of salivary gland **lymphoma** (nodal or
  parenchymal MALT lymphoma arising in Sjögren's-affected glands) was identified.
  An analogous CEUS-of-lymphomatous-lymph-nodes study (PMC6364116) reported fast-in
  hyperenhancement patterns (83.1% of lesions) but concerns cervical lymph nodes
  generally, **not** salivary parenchyma specifically — usable only as indirect/
  analog evidence, not direct salivary-gland lymphoma CEUS data.
- **Explicit gap:** no CEUS series specifically characterizing Sjögren's syndrome
  glandular involvement, MALT lymphoma arising within salivary glands, or acute/
  chronic sialadenitis enhancement patterns (beyond the single case-based Mikulicz
  report above) was found in this search.

## 6. Standardization / reproducibility / vendor dependence

- No salivary-gland-specific CEUS reproducibility, inter-observer, or
  inter-vendor/inter-software study was identified. **Explicit gap.**
- General (non-organ-specific) CEUS quantification reproducibility literature
  exists — e.g., a QIBA (Quantitative Imaging Biomarker Alliance)-affiliated study,
  "Evaluation of the Reproducibility of Bolus Transit Quantification With
  Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasound Across Multiple Scanners and Analysis Software
  Packages," *Investigative Radiology* (via Ovid listing) — **VERIFIED
  (single clear listing, title/journal consistent)** for existence, but this is
  a general-CEUS methodology paper, **not salivary-gland-specific**; cite only
  as background on the known problem of scanner/software variability, not as
  salivary-gland validation.
- The salivary-gland quantitative studies identified (Klotz 2014, Wakonig 2023)
  consistently used **VueBox (Bracco, Italy)** as external perfusion-analysis
  software, implying vendor/software dependence is a latent methodological issue
  in this literature (different studies may use different scanners/software with
  unreported cross-comparability), but no paper was found that directly tested
  this for salivary gland CEUS.

## 7. Regulatory status / real-world clinical implementation

- General (non-salivary-specific) facts, multi-source confirmed as common
  background knowledge: SonoVue (sulfur hexafluoride microbubble contrast) is
  widely available and used in Europe and Asia; the same or related agent is
  marketed as Lumason in North America.
- A general EFSUMB-associated statement retrieved via search: caution should be
  exercised regarding off-label use of gas-containing second-generation
  ultrasound contrast agents "in tissues/patients in which damage to the
  microvasculature may have serious clinical implications" — this is a **general
  non-hepatic CEUS caveat**, not a salivary-gland-specific regulatory statement.
- **Explicit gap / verification limit:** I could not confirm from primary sources
  whether salivary gland CEUS is formally on-label or off-label for any specific
  contrast agent in the EU, UK, or USA. This requires direct consultation of
  product labeling (SmPC for SonoVue/Lumason) and/or the full EFSUMB guideline
  text, neither of which was accessible in this session due to egress
  restrictions. **Do not state a definitive regulatory/label claim in the
  manuscript without independent confirmation.**

## 8. 2023–2026 systematic reviews / meta-analyses

- **Rogalska et al. 2022** (J Clin Med 11(24):7360) — technically published

  and reported in *Student's Journal of Health Research Africa* (December 2024).
  — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** for essentially all bibliographic detail
  (exact title, full author list, volume/issue, DOI were not retrieved beyond
  the journal name and general scope). **Additional caution:** this journal is
  not one I could confirm as indexed in MEDLINE/Scopus/Web of Science within

  verified before citing it in a submission-track manuscript — treat as a
  provisional/low-confidence lead only, to be replaced with full verification or
  dropped if it cannot be confirmed as a legitimate indexed source.
  Reported takeaway (unverified numbers): CEUS features such as blurred
  enhancement margin and increased lesion size aid malignancy risk assessment;
  no pooled sensitivity/specificity figure was captured.
- **Wakonig et al., Head & Neck, 2026** (item in §4) is the most recent
  (2026) primary study identified, though it is a diagnostic-pathway study,
  not a systematic review/meta-analysis.
- **No 2023, 2025, or 2026 systematic review or meta-analysis with pooled
  diagnostic-accuracy statistics (sensitivity/specificity/AUC) specifically for
  CEUS in malignant-vs-benign salivary gland tumor differentiation was
  confirmed with adequate bibliographic detail.** This is an explicit, notable
  gap in the current literature base as identified in this session — the
  evidence base for CEUS's overall diagnostic accuracy in salivary tumors
  appears to rest on single-center cohort studies and one PA-vs-WT-focused
  perfusion-parameter meta-analysis (Rogalska 2022), rather than a
  malignancy-focused pooled accuracy meta-analysis.

## 10. Summary of explicit gaps

   blocked); only inferred as "plausibly included" among superficial-organ
   applications.

   CEUS distinguishing malignant from benign salivary gland tumors was

   Sjögren's-syndrome glandular CEUS characterization was found (only one
   case-based IgG4/Mikulicz report and an indirect cervical-lymph-node-lymphoma

7. Regulatory/label status (on-label vs off-label) of CEUS contrast agents for

8. Several author-name and DOI details (items flagged UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE

## 11. Verification-limits note (repeat, for reviewer transparency)

Every claim above was cross-checked against WebSearch result *listings*
(titles/URLs from PubMed, PMC, MDPI, Wiley, Frontiers, ScienceDirect,
ResearchGate, Springer, medultrason.ro, and efsumb.org) rather than by
independently reading full text or abstracts directly, because WebFetch to all
attempted academic domains (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov,
www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, europepmc.org, semanticscholar.org, doi.org,
onlinelibrary.wiley.com, www.frontiersin.org, www.thieme-connect.com) returned
EGRESS_BLOCKED (network/organizational policy denial) on every attempt in this
session. "VERIFIED (multi-source)" therefore means *consistent bibliographic
identity across independent search-result listings*, not independently-read
full text. Numeric findings attributed to "VERIFIED" citations were themselves
often transcribed only from AI-generated WebSearch summary paragraphs, not from
directly read tables — treat all numeric results in this document as requiring
one more confirmation pass against primary full text before final manuscript
submission.
