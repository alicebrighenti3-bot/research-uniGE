## 2. Key comparative / diagnostic-accuracy studies

### Conventional color/power Doppler — benign vs malignant tumor discrimination

4. **Bradley MJ, Durham LH, Lancer JM.** "The role of colour flow Doppler in the investigation of the salivary gland tumour." *Clin Radiol.* 2000;55(10):759–762. PMID 11052876. **VERIFIED** (recurred across 2 queries with matching journal/volume/pages). n=56 patients; PSV, PI, RI measured on pulsed-wave Doppler. Reported conclusion (per search synthesis, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for the exact numbers**): RI > 0.8 and PI > 1.8 associated with malignancy; sensitivity 75.5%, specificity 85.7%, PPV 97.3%. This is the most frequently cited "classic" spectral-Doppler cutoff paper for salivary tumors in the retrieved literature.

5. **Dumitriu D, et al.** "B-mode and color Doppler ultrasound features of salivary gland tumors." *Medical Ultrasonography.* 2008;10(1):31–37 (Romanian journal; URL-confirmed volume/issue/pages). **VERIFIED** for journal/year/volume/pages (recurred across 2 queries); full author list **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**. Reported finding (synthesis only): ~61% of benign and ~56% of malignant tumors were poorly vascularized on color Doppler, authors noting most malignant cases were early-stage — an explicit **discordant/negative** finding relative to the "malignant = hypervascular/high-resistance" paradigm.

Authors **not identified** in this search (my targeted author-guess query returned no match). **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — title/journal only; treat as provisional until author/volume/page/DOI can be confirmed via full-text access.

7. **Davachi B, Imanimoghaddam M, Majidi MR, Sahebalam A, Johari M, Javadian Langaroodi A, Shakeri MT.** "The Efficacy of Magnetic Resonance Imaging and Color Doppler Ultrasonography in Diagnosis of Salivary Gland Tumors." *J Dent Res Dent Clin Dent Prospects.* 2014. ### Superb Microvascular Imaging (SMI) / microvascular US — salivary gland tumors

14. **Ryoo I, Suh S, Lee YH, Seol HY, Woo JS, Kim SC, Seo HS.** (author order as returned; not independently cross-checked against the journal's own author-order listing) "Vascular Pattern Analysis on Microvascular Sonography for Differentiation of Pleomorphic Adenomas and Warthin Tumors of Salivary Glands." *J Ultrasound Med.* 2018 Mar;37(3):613–620. DOI 10.1002/jum.14368. PMID 28885737. **VERIFIED** (recurred across 3 independent queries with identical journal/year/volume/issue/pages/DOI/PMID). n=62 (38 pleomorphic adenoma, 24 Warthin tumor); conclusion: microvascular-sonography vascular-pattern analysis, combined with other B-mode features, aids PA-vs-WT differentiation — clinically relevant because management differs (partial parotidectomy for PA vs enucleation feasible for WT). **Gap**: the specific vascular-pattern categories/percentages used in this paper were not retrievable from snippets (distinct from ref. 15/16 below, which report different, apparently independent, percentage data).

15. **Zhao L, Mao Y, Mu J, Xin X** (author names as returned by search synthesis; **not independently corroborated** by a second source — flag). DOI not retrieved. **VERIFIED** for journal/year/article-number/PMID (recurred across 4 independent queries); author names **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**.

16. **"The diagnostic value of Superb Microvascular Imaging in identifying benign tumors of parotid gland."** *BMC Med Imaging.* 2020;20 [exact article/page number not retrieved]. DOI 10.1186/s12880-020-00506-y. PMCID PMC7493138. **VERIFIED** for journal/year/DOI/PMCID (recurred across 3 queries). **Important caution**: refs 15 and 16 report near-identical study designs, sample composition (pleomorphic adenoma / Warthin tumor / basal cell adenoma), and near-identical findings (vascular index [VI] and microvessel density [MVD] both lowest in pleomorphic adenoma, highest in Warthin tumor; VI correlates with MVD; SMI outperforms CDFI for vascular distribution/vascularity assessment). Reported quantitative detail (synthesis, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**): 249 parotid masses/217 patients; pleomorphic adenoma mainly peripheral (33.1%) or avascular (25.7%) distribution, SMI grade 0–1 predominant; Warthin tumor mainly central (31.3%)/mixed (34.9%) distribution, SMI grade 2–3 predominant; basal cell adenoma mainly peripheral (33.3%)/mixed (33.3%), grade 2–3.

### Adjacent/comparator: SMI in cervical lymph nodes (not salivary-gland-specific)

18. **Sim JK/JY (initials inconsistent across snippets — likely "Sim JK"), Lee JY, Hong HS.** "Differentiation Between Malignant and Benign Lymph Nodes: Role of Superb Microvascular Imaging in the Evaluation of Cervical Lymph Nodes." *J Ultrasound Med.* 2019;38(11):3025–3036. DOI 10.1002/jum.15010. **VERIFIED** (recurred across 2 queries with consistent volume/issue/pages/DOI). Findings (synthesis, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for exact figures**): SMI sensitivity for malignant-vs-benign nodes 86.9% vs 54.1% for power Doppler US (p<0.001); benign vascularity = hilar/parallel branching vessels; malignant = aberrant peripheral/subcapsular, dysplastic vessels. General cervical lymph node population, **not restricted to salivary-region or parotid/submandibular nodal basins** — cite as adjacent evidence for the SMI-nodal-evaluation angle the user requested, with explicit caveat that it is not salivary-gland-specific.

## 3. Systematic reviews / meta-analyses

19. **Liu Y, Li J, Tan YR, Xiong P, Zhong LP.** "Accuracy of diagnosis of salivary gland tumors with the use of ultrasonography, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging: a meta-analysis." *Oral Surg Oral Med Oral Pathol Oral Radiol.* 2015;119(2):238–245. DOI 10.1016/j.oooo.2014.10.020. PMID 25577417. **VERIFIED** (recurred across 2 queries with consistent numbers). 19 studies pooled. Pooled sensitivity/specificity: US 0.629/0.920; CT 0.830/0.851; MRI 0.807/0.886. **Important scope caveat: this meta-analysis addresses grayscale-US diagnostic accuracy for benign/malignant discrimination generally — it is not Doppler/vascularity-specific**, and Doppler parameters are not disaggregated in the pooled analysis per available snippets.

20. **"Diagnostic accuracy of ultrasound superb microvascular imaging for parotid tumors: A protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis."** PMCID PMC7850700 (format consistent with a *Medicine (Baltimore)* PRISMA-P registered protocol, ~2021, based on typical journal conventions for this PMC ID range — **not confirmed**, journal name/authors not retrieved). **Directly answers the user's explicit ask ("2023-2026 systematic reviews... on Doppler or microvascular US of salivary glands") but only as a protocol.** No completed/published systematic review matching this protocol's stated aim (SMI diagnostic accuracy for benign vs malignant parotid tumors) was identified anywhere in this search. **This is the single most important explicit gap for the review**: either (a) the completed review was never published, (b) it was published under a different, unretrieved title, or (c) it exists but did not surface in these queries — full-text/registry (PROSPERO) check strongly recommended before the review is drafted.

21. **"Classification of Salivary Gland Tumors on Ultrasound Using Artificial Intelligence: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."** *Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg* (OHNS/AAO-HNSF journals). DOI 10.1002/ohn.70102. Authors per synthesis: Chau IJ, Kim CHS, Nguyen SA, McCray LR, Murdaugh JD, Newman JG, et al. — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** for full author list; year/volume/pages not retrieved. 12 studies, n=4721; pooled sensitivity/specificity generally >0.80, AUC approaching 0.90 (synthesis figure, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**). **Scope caveat: this is an AI/machine-learning classification review, not a Doppler/SMI-specific review** — cite only as adjacent evidence that ultrasound-based (mostly B-mode ± some vascularity features) classification is an active 2023–2026 research area; does not fulfill the user's specific ask for a Doppler/MVI systematic review.

22. **Sjögren's syndrome salivary gland US meta-analyses** (adjacent, diffuse-disease scope, not tumor-Doppler-specific): (a) *Sci Rep.* 2018, pooled sensitivity 80%/specificity 90% across 54 studies/6087 patients (scoring-system meta-analysis, grayscale-focused); (b) Ramsubeik K, Motilal S, Sanchez-Ramos L, Ramrattan LA, Kaeley GS, Singh JA, *Ther Adv Musculoskelet Dis* 2020 — title and journal recurred consistently, **VERIFIED** for existence/topic, numeric pooled estimates not independently re-confirmed in this session. Neither is centered on Doppler/vascularity parameters for glandular disease.

**No systematic review or meta-analysis specifically pooling Doppler spectral parameters (RI/PI/PSV) or SMI/MVI quantitative vascular indices for benign-vs-malignant salivary gland tumor discrimination was found as a completed, published work.** The closest match (ref. 20) is an unfulfilled protocol.

## 4. Explicit gaps identified

- **No completed systematic review/meta-analysis on Doppler or SMI/MVI specifically for salivary gland tumor characterization** was located (see ref. 20) — despite this being the user's central request. Recommend a direct PROSPERO/Medicine-journal check outside this session's blocked-egress constraints.
- **No dedicated technical-optimization literature (PRF, wall filter, gain) specific to salivary gland Doppler/SMI acquisition** was found; only generic low-flow Doppler engineering principles from unrelated domains (patents, obstetric 3D/4D power Doppler quantification) surfaced. This is either a genuine literature gap or a search-strategy limitation (budget exhausted before deeper technical-parameter queries could run) — cannot distinguish the two from this session.
- **Discordant/unattributed RI, PI, and PSV cutoff values** appeared in early broad searches (e.g., "RI cutoff 0.76, sensitivity 80%, specificity 88.9%, AUC 0.82"; "benign RI 0.65 vs malignant 0.81"; "benign RI 0.77 vs malignant 0.86, p=0.021"; "PSV 22.15 cm/s benign vs 32.74 cm/s malignant"; "malignant PI 1.52 vs benign 1.25, cutoff 1.28, sensitivity 80%/specificity 77.8%") **without clean, confirmed attribution to specific individual papers** beyond Bradley 2000 (ref. 4). These numbers should **not** be used in the review without first tracing each to a verifiable source — they may originate from refs 4, 5, 6, or others conflated by the search synthesis.
- **Vascular-pattern/percentage data for refs 15 and 16 (SMI in PA/WT/BCA) may represent a single study reported twice**, not two independent confirmatory studies — needs resolution before use as convergent evidence (see caution under ref. 16).
- **No MV-Flow (Samsung), MicroV (Canon), or other vendor-specific slow-flow Doppler technology study in salivary gland pathology** was found; MV-Flow literature identified was confined to musculoskeletal, abdominal, placental, and prostate applications. This appears to be a genuine literature gap — SMI (Canon/Toshiba) dominates the salivary-gland microvascular-imaging literature almost exclusively; cross-vendor comparative data are absent.
- **Malignant subtype-specific vascular pattern data** (adenoid cystic carcinoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, salivary duct carcinoma) on conventional Doppler or SMI could not be searched before the session's WebSearch quota (200/200) was exhausted — flagged as an unexecuted search, not a confirmed literature gap.
- **SMI vs conventional Doppler in salivary-region/parotid nodal (not generic cervical) lymph node evaluation** specifically was not found; only generic cervical lymph node (ref. 18) and thyroid-cancer-nodal-basin SMI literature surfaced.
- Full text of nearly every reference above was inaccessible in this session (egress-blocked), so **quantitative details, exact author order, and some journal/DOI fields could not be independently confirmed against the primary source** — treat all "UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE" tags as requiring confirmation via PubMed/Crossref/journal access before submission-grade citation.
