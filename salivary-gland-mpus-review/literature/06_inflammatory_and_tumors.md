## PART A — Inflammatory / Obstructive Salivary Gland Disease

### A1. Sialadenitis (acute, chronic, recurrent) — general US reviews

1. **Koch M, Sievert M, Iro H, Mantsopoulos K, Schapher M.** "Ultrasound in Inflammatory and Obstructive Salivary Gland Diseases: Own Experiences and a Review of the Literature." *J Clin Med.* 2021 Aug 12;10(16):3547. DOI: 10.3390/jcm10163547. PMID: 34441850.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: appeared with consistent authors/journal/DOI/PMID across 2 independent searches; also indexed on PMC as PMC8397054).
   Key points reported: US is an established first-line tool for obstructive disease (sialolithiasis, tumors); fewer robust data exist for pure inflammatory (non-obstructive) disease; US reported useful in >90% of cases presenting with inflammatory/obstructive salivary gland disease. Acute sialadenitis: glandular swelling, hypoechoic/heterogeneous parenchyma with hypo-/anechoic foci, duct dilation in bacterial/purulent forms, increased color/power Doppler flow. Chronic sialadenitis: heterogeneous echogenicity reflecting fibrosis, ductal dilation, glandular atrophy.

2. **Resende EA, Gomes NR, Abreu LG, Castro MAA, Aguiar MCF.** "The applicability of ultrasound in the diagnosis of inflammatory and obstructive diseases of the major salivary glands: a scoping review." *Dentomaxillofac Radiol.* 2022 May 1;51(4):20210361. DOI: 10.1259/dmfr.20210361. PMID: 34762496.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: appeared in 2 independent searches with consistent authors/journal/volume/DOI/PMID; also on PMC as PMC9499200).

3. **Juvenile recurrent parotitis (JRP)** — described (via several search snippets, not one single pinned primary source) as showing scattered hypoechoic foci ("Swiss cheese"/"moth-eaten" pattern), hypervascularity in acute phase and hypovascularity in chronic phase on Doppler. Related systematic review found: "Sialendoscopy approach in treating juvenile recurrent parotitis: a systematic review," *J Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg* (Springer), PMC10440031/PMC10649241.
   **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** for the specific US morphologic description (authors, exact PMID/DOI of the systematic review, and the primary source of the "Swiss cheese" descriptor were not independently re-confirmed after the first search pass — flagged as a gap, not to be treated as a fully pinned citation).

### A2. Sialolithiasis — diagnostic accuracy of US

4. **Kim DH, Kang JM, Kim SW, Kim SH, Jung JH, Hwang SH.** "Utility of Ultrasonography for Diagnosis of Salivary Gland Sialolithiasis: A Meta-Analysis." *Laryngoscope.* 2022 Sep;132(9):1785–1791. DOI: 10.1002/lary.30020. PMID: 35043982.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: consistent authors, journal, volume/pages, DOI, PMID across 2 independent searches).
   Meta-analysis of 10 studies, 1393 patients. Diagnostic odds ratio 162.60 (95% CI 53.99–489.72); AUC of summary ROC = 0.963. Parotid-gland US reported to show the highest diagnostic accuracy among subgroups (vs. submandibular). Reference standards: sialendoscopy, sialography, or surgery.

5. **Terraz S, Poletti PA, Dulguerov P, Dfouni N, Becker CD, Marchal F, Becker M.** "How reliable is sonography in the assessment of sialolithiasis?" *AJR Am J Roentgenol.* 2013 Jul;201(1):W104–9. DOI: 10.2214/AJR.12.9383. PMID: 23789681.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: appeared with consistent authors/journal/PMID/DOI across 3 independent searches).
   Sensitivity 77%, specificity 95%, accuracy 85%, PPV 94%, NPV 78% for sonographic detection of calculi.

6. **Goncalves M, Schapher M, Iro H, Wuest W, Mantsopoulos K, Koch M.** "Value of Sonography in the Diagnosis of Sialolithiasis: Comparison With the Reference Standard of Direct Stone Identification." *J Ultrasound Med.* 2017 Nov;36(11):2227–2235 (Epub 2017 May 27). PMID: 28556090.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: title/journal/authors appeared in 2 independent searches; numeric detail from single synthesis pass but internally consistent with the article's stated design).
   659 glands with obstructive sialadenopathy; sensitivity 94.7%, specificity 97.4%, PPV 99.4%, NPV 79.6% (direct stone identification as reference standard — high-accuracy outlier relative to other studies, likely reflecting expert single-center setting).

7. **Özçelik N, Vehbi H, Alaskarov E.** "Comparison of the Inadequacies of Ultrasonography and Computed Tomography in the Diagnosis of Sialolithiasis." *Ear Nose Throat J* (Sage). 2024. DOI: 10.1177/01455613241287291.
   **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**, and internally inconsistent across the retrieved snippet: one part states US sensitivity 65%/specificity 80% vs. CT 98%/88% (sialendoscopy as reference); another part of the same search-result synthesis states sialendoscopy sensitivity 100% "significantly higher than US (86%) and CT (95%)" — these two sets of numbers do not reconcile (65% vs. 86% for US sensitivity) and likely reflect either two different subgroups (parotid vs. submandibular) or a synthesis error. **Reported here with explicit caution; figures should be re-verified directly against the primary article before use in the review**, and the discrepancy should not be silently resolved.

8. **Parotid gland sialolithiasis: a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis.** *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol.* 2025. DOI: 10.1007/s00405-025-09697-y. PMID: 41034633.
   **VERIFIED** (multi-source: appeared in 2 independent searches with consistent DOI/PMID/journal/year). Author names not captured in any retrieved snippet — **gap**.
   PRISMA-based review of parotid stone treatment modalities (endoscopy-only, endoscopy-assisted, ESWL); focused on treatment stone-free rate/complications rather than diagnostic US accuracy per se — relevant mainly as a companion reference for the sialolithiasis management context, not for US diagnostic performance data.

9. **Point-of-care US case reports (sialolithiasis/sialadenitis):**
   - Huang F, Caton R, Colla J. "Point-of-care Ultrasound Diagnosis of Acute Sialolithiasis with Sialadenitis." *Clin Pract Cases Emerg Med.* 2017 Oct 18. DOI: 10.5811/cpcem.2017.7.34907. PMC5965241.
     **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent authors/DOI/PMC ID). Illustrative case: 7.3 mm submandibular sialolith, hyperemic gland on POCUS.
   - Kramer JA, Pourmirzaie R. "Utility of Point-of-Care Ultrasound for the Rapid Evaluation of Acute Sialadenitis: A Case Report." *Cureus.* 2022 May 10. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.24881. PMC9182128.
     **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent authors/DOI/PMC ID).
   These are single case reports illustrating POCUS utility, not diagnostic-accuracy studies — cite as illustrative/educational evidence only, not as accuracy data.

### A3. Ductal stenosis

10. General finding (multiple independent search snippets, consistent): US is proposed as a reasonable first-line tool when duct stenosis/stricture is suspected — it can identify pre-stenotic ductal dilation and localize the transition point, but **failure to visualize a dilated duct does not exclude stricture**; sialendoscopy remains the reference standard for final diagnosis and characterization of stenosis, and is also therapeutic (gland preservation achieved in >90% of appropriately selected cases per general sialendoscopy literature).
    Related title found: "Salivary duct stenosis: diagnosis and treatment." PMID: 28516976. **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — journal, authors, volume/pages not captured/confirmed; **gap**.
    Related title: "Stenoses of the salivary ducts – sialendoscopy based diagnosis and treatment" (ScienceDirect). **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — full citation (authors, journal, year, PMID/DOI) not captured; **gap**.

### A4. Abscess formation

11. General US findings (consistent across several independent, non-peer-reviewed/educational sources — Radiology Key, Iowa Head & Neck Protocols, and one peer-reviewed source below): heterogeneous collection with anechoic center and isoechoic/hyperechoic rim, increased peripheral vascularity on Doppler; contrasts with normal homogeneous, mildly hyperechoic parotid parenchyma.
    "The role of ultrasound in evaluating salivary gland swellings." *Egypt J Otolaryngol* (Springer). **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — authors, year, volume, PMID/DOI not captured; **gap**.
    US-guided needle aspiration reported as a safe, effective, less invasive alternative to surgical incision/drainage for parotid abscess (MDPI J Clin Med comparative study identified in search but full citation — authors, PMID/DOI — **not captured; gap**).

### A5. Sjögren-associated (autoimmune) sialadenitis — OMERACT US scoring (adjacent/relevant context)

12. OMERACT ultrasound grayscale (0–3) and Doppler (0–3) semiquantitative scoring system for major salivary gland involvement in primary Sjögren syndrome. Multiple related PMIDs surfaced (31036626, 31601631, 33879581, 34849616, 36893528) across a cluster of validation/reliability studies; one report notes a total-score cutoff of 8 yielding sensitivity 76%, specificity 90%, AUC 0.828 for diagnostic value vs. reference standards.
    **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for individual full citations** (author lists not reliably attributed to specific PMIDs in the retrieved snippets) — **flagged as a cluster of real, indexed publications whose exact author/journal/PMID pairing needs primary-source confirmation before citing individually.** This topic is adjacent to chronic sialadenitis and may be more appropriate for a separate autoimmune/Sjögren-focused section of the review rather than PART A as scoped here.

13. **Hoffman HT, et al. (Salivary Gland Committee, American Academy of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery).** "Proposal for standardized ultrasound analysis of the salivary glands: Part 1 submandibular gland." *Laryngoscope Investig Otolaryngol.* 2024 Feb. PMID: 38362174.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source: appeared in 2 independent searches with consistent PMID/journal/date; full author list and exact volume/page numbers **not captured — gap**). International multidisciplinary expert-opinion effort to standardize US terminology/technique for submandibular gland reporting.

## PART B — Benign and Malignant Focal Tumors (B-mode/Doppler characterization)

### B1. Benign tumors — morphologic US features

14. **Yuan WH, Hsu HC, Chou YH, Hsueh HC, Tseng TK, Tiu CM.** "Gray-scale and color Doppler ultrasonographic features of pleomorphic adenoma and Warthin's tumor in major salivary glands." *Clin Imaging.* 2009 Sep-Oct;33(5):348–353. DOI: 10.1016/j.clinimag.2008.12.004. PMID: 19712813.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent full citation).
    Reported findings: lobulated shape more frequent in pleomorphic adenoma (PA, 84.2%) than Warthin tumor (WT, 51.7%); anechoic cystic components far more common in WT (44.8%) than PA (5.3%); vascularity — PA predominantly grade 0–1 (77.9%), WT predominantly grade 2–3 (73.1%), with WT flow more often central/mixed and PA more often peripheral (53.2%). Note: one synthesis pass also stated "no grayscale features showed significant difference" in a different (unnamed) study — **internal inconsistency across sources, flagged**; grayscale differentiation is clearly imperfect and vascular/Doppler features are reported as more discriminating than grayscale alone.

15. **Rong X, Zhu Q, Ji H, Li J, Huang H.** "Differentiation of pleomorphic adenoma and Warthin's tumor of the parotid gland: ultrasonographic features." *Acta Radiol.* 2014. DOI: 10.1177/0284185113515865. PMID: 24324278.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source: title/PMID/DOI appeared consistently in 2 independent searches). Exact volume/pages **not captured — gap**.

16. **Ryoo H, et al.** "Vascular Pattern Analysis on Microvascular Sonography for Differentiation of Pleomorphic Adenomas and Warthin Tumors of Salivary Glands." *J Ultrasound Med.* 2018. DOI: 10.1002/jum.14368. PMID: 28885737.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 3 independent searches, consistent DOI/PMID/journal). Full author list, volume/issue/pages **not captured — gap**. This is a microvascular-imaging (superb microvascular imaging-type) study — flag for cross-check with the elastography/CEUS/MVI-focused companion review to avoid duplication.

17. **Rogalska M, Antkowiak L, Kasperczuk A, Scierski W.** "Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasound in the Differentiation between the Most Common Benign Parotid Gland Tumors: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *J Clin Med.* 2022 Dec 12;11(24):7360. DOI: 10.3390/jcm11247360. PMID: 36555976.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent full citation).
    15 studies included; PAs showed more heterogeneous CEUS enhancement texture than WTs; longer normalized mean transit time and time-to-peak in PA; higher AUC and time-from-peak-to-half in WT; enhancement margin/rim/lesion size did not differ significantly between the two entities. **This is a CEUS paper, not B-mode/Doppler** — noted here per instructions as an example of integrated mpUS literature, but full analysis belongs to the companion elastography/CEUS/MVI review.

18. **Oncocytoma / basal cell adenoma / lipoma / cysts:** General (multi-source but non-specific) findings — oncocytoma on US described as a hypoechoic, well-defined mass with no features reliably distinguishing it from other benign parotid tumors (Warthin tumor, basal cell adenoma, PA); marked overlap in grayscale appearance across benign entity types is repeatedly noted. Basal cell adenoma: benign tumor most common in parotid, may show cystic degeneration on imaging (one non-US-specific pathology source noted 30.23% cystic change in a 43-case series — imaging modality of that series **not confirmed as US; likely CT/MRI-based — flagged, do not attribute to sonography without primary-source check**).
    **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE / GAP:** no dedicated, well-characterized primary US-morphology study for basal cell adenoma, lipoma, or simple/benign cysts was retrieved with a confirmable PMID/DOI in this search pass. This is a genuine literature gap for the review to flag — most oncocytoma/basal cell adenoma imaging literature identified emphasizes CT/MRI or is case-report level, not systematic US-morphology series.

19. **Benign lymphoepithelial lesions (HIV-associated):** "Benign lymphoepithelial parotid lesions in HIV-positive patients: spectrum of findings at gray-scale and Doppler sonography." PMID: 7677004.
    **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — authors, journal, year, volume/pages, DOI not captured/confirmed in this search pass; PMID format (7-digit, no leading zeros as typically seen for mid-1990s indexing) is plausible for a mid-1990s article but **should be independently verified before citation**. Reported features (from search synthesis, same caveat): cystic lesions with thin internal septa and vascular pedicles ± solid mural nodule; "mixed" solid-cystic nodules more hypervascular than purely cystic ones; low-resistance arterial flow (RI 0.36–0.70); sonographic appearance explicitly described as non-specific, especially for mixed nodules resembling neoplasm.

### B2. Malignant tumors — US features and limitations for histologic prediction

20. **Rzepakowska A, Osuch-Wójcikiewicz E, Sobol M, Cruz R, Sielska-Badurek E, Niemczyk K.** "The differential diagnosis of parotid gland tumors with high-resolution ultrasound in otolaryngological practice." *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol.* 2017 Jun 13. DOI: 10.1007/s00405-017-4636-2. PMC5500678.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent authors/DOI/PMC ID). PMID **not captured — gap**.
    72 consecutively resected parotid tumors, 12 MHz probe + color Doppler, histopathologic confirmation. Benign-vs-malignant differentiation: sensitivity 60%, specificity 95.2%, accuracy 90.3%, PPV 66.8%, NPV 93.6%. **Low sensitivity is the key limitation** — grayscale/Doppler US misses a substantial minority of malignancies (40% false-negative rate for malignancy in this series), even though specificity is high.

21. **Wang Y, Nie F, Wang P, Wang L.** "Diagnostic grading of parotid lesions by conventional ultrasound: a pilot study." *Dentomaxillofac Radiol.* 2022;51(4):20210484. PMC9499195.
    **VERIFIED** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent authors/journal/volume). PMID **not captured — gap**.
    Retrospective, 150 patients, two independent observers; 7 morphologic patterns grouped into an 8-tier grading scheme (Grade 0 = non-diagnostic image quality, up to Grade 7 = "already malignant-appearing") intended to stratify malignancy probability by conventional grayscale US alone.

22. General grayscale malignancy-sign synthesis (multiple independent search snippets, broadly consistent but with important caveats): irregular shape, poorly defined margins, marked heterogeneous echogenicity, and moderate-to-high vascularity are the classically cited malignancy predictors. However, one retrieved dataset explicitly notes **margins were well-defined in 100% of benign tumors but poorly defined in only ~30% of malignant tumors** — i.e., most malignant tumors in that series still had reasonably well-defined margins, undercutting "irregular margin" as a reliably sensitive sign. Similarly, shape was irregular in "over 50%" of both benign (PA) and malignant tumors in the same synthesis — **an internal contradiction with the "irregular shape = malignant" heuristic that must be flagged rather than smoothed over.** Primary source(s) for this specific dataset were not individually re-confirmed with PMID/DOI in this pass — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, explicit overlap/caution flag for the review text.**

23. **AI-based systematic review (useful as a benchmark for "how good is conventional/non-AI US alone"):** Chau IJ, Kim CH, Nguyen SA, McCray LR, Murdaugh JD, Newman JG. "Classification of salivary gland tumors on ultrasound using artificial intelligence: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg.* 2026;174:601–612. DOI: 10.1002/ohn.70102. PROSPERO CRD420250651068. 12 studies, N=4721 (search conducted through Jan 28, 2025).
    **VERIFIED for existence/core citation** (multi-source, 2 independent searches, consistent authors/DOI/journal/volume/pages/PROSPERO ID). PMID **not captured — gap** (may be very recently indexed/ahead-of-print at time of search).
    **The specific figure "conventional US without AI: specificity 92%, sensitivity 66%" appeared only once, in an unattributed synthesis sentence, and could not be independently cross-confirmed against a second search pass — mark this number UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE and verify against the primary article before use.** If accurate, it would support the theme (echoed elsewhere in this file) that conventional grayscale/Doppler US is more specific than sensitive for malignancy — i.e., a "reassuring" negative US does not reliably exclude a malignant tumor, while high specificity means clearly malignant-appearing lesions are usually correctly flagged.

24. **Mucoepidermoid carcinoma / adenoid cystic carcinoma / acinic cell carcinoma:** No dedicated, systematic B-mode/Doppler morphology study specific to these three histotypes (as distinct from generic "malignant parotid tumor" features) was retrieved with a confirmable citation. Retrieved literature on this cluster was predominantly **cytology-based** (Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology performance: reported overall diagnostic accuracy 76.6% for mucoepidermoid carcinoma, 71.8% for acinic cell carcinoma, 88.9% for adenoid cystic carcinoma) rather than sonomorphologic-feature-based — full citation (authors/journal/year/PMID/DOI) for this Milan System accuracy figure **not captured — gap**, and figure is **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**. **This is an explicit, genuine literature gap: histotype-specific grayscale/Doppler US signatures for MEC/AdCC/ACC are not well established in the indexed literature identified here** — these tumors typically share the generic hypoechoic, variably-marginated, variably-vascular appearance of "malignant parotid mass" without reliable US features that predict specific histology.

25. **Salivary duct carcinoma / carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma:** No dedicated US-morphology primary study was found. Retrieved sources were pathology-overview and case-report level (e.g., a case report of carcinoma ex-PA of the parotid, PMC7501749 and related). Clinical pearl surfaced from search: carcinoma ex-PA classically presents as a long-standing parotid mass with recent rapid growth ± pain ± facial palsy — a history-based red flag rather than a sonographic sign. **Explicit gap: no confirmable primary US-imaging series for carcinoma ex-PA or salivary duct carcinoma was retrieved.**

26. **Metastases to salivary glands:** General synthesis (multi-source, broadly consistent): intraparotid lymph nodes are the most common route/site of parotid metastasis; cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head/scalp/face is the most frequently cited primary; ~60% of parotid metastases originate from head-and-neck primaries (skin melanoma, nasopharyngeal, thyroid cancers cited). US appearance of metastatic node involvement described as often solid, hypoechoic, ill-defined/irregular margins, with a tendency toward central necrosis, and possibly multiple nodes. "Role of Intra-Parotid Lymph Node Metastasis in Primary Parotid Carcinoma," PMC9781477, and "Primary and Secondary Tumors of the Parotid Gland: Clinical Features and Prognosis," PMC9954225 — **both UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**, full citations (authors/journal/year/PMID/DOI) **not captured — gap**.

27. **Lymphoma (parotid, MALT-type):** General synthesis (single search pass): sonographic patterns described as "linear echogenic strands" or multiple small hypoechoic nodules in a "tortoiseshell/segmental" pattern for MALT lymphoma involving salivary glands. **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — no specific primary citation (authors/journal/PMID/DOI) was retrieved for this descriptor; **explicit gap**.

### B3. Lymph node evaluation (intraparotid and cervical)

28. General teaching-level synthesis on reactive vs. metastatic vs. lymphomatous nodes (multi-source but not tied to a single high-quality primary citation): reactive nodes — oval shape, preserved echogenic hilum, indistinct/unsharp border, no necrosis, hilar/central vascularity. Metastatic nodes — rounder shape, loss of/effaced hilum, sharper border, possible intranodal necrosis or calcification, peripheral/aberrant vascularity. Loss of echogenic hilum reported as the most sensitive single sign; short-axis/long-axis ratio reported as the most specific parameter. One source (Hindawi *ISRN*, 2014, 45 subjects across 3 groups of 15) specifically evaluated US differentiation of benign vs. metastatic cervical nodes — **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**, authors/exact journal/PMID/DOI **not captured — gap**.
    Important overlap caveat also surfaced: metastatic, lymphomatous, and even tuberculous nodes can retain a visible echogenic hilum in early-stage involvement (before medullary sinus architecture is disrupted) — i.e., **hilar preservation does not reliably exclude malignant/lymphomatous involvement**, a limitation worth explicit mention in the review.
    Size criteria cited (single synthesis pass, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**): normal cervical nodes ~0.82 cm mean short axis, reactive ~1.24 cm, metastatic ~2.29 cm; general teaching threshold of >1 cm short axis as "abnormal," with explicit acknowledgment in the literature that **no single universal size cutoff reliably distinguishes benign from neoplastic nodes**.

### B4. Recent systematic reviews / meta-analyses (2023–2026) on benign vs. malignant US differentiation

29. Chau IJ et al. 2026 AI systematic review (see #23 above) — most recent (2026, searches through Jan 2025) systematic review identified that explicitly frames conventional (non-AI) US diagnostic performance as a comparator arm.
30. Rogalska et al. 2022 CEUS systematic review (see #17) — technically outside the 2023–2026 window but closely adjacent and frequently cited in newer papers.
    **No additional 2023–2026 systematic review or meta-analysis specifically on grayscale/Doppler (non-contrast, non-AI) US differentiation of benign vs. malignant salivary gland tumors was identified with a confirmable citation in this search pass.** This itself is worth noting as a gap: most recent systematic-review activity in this space (2023–2026) is concentrated on AI/radiomics and CEUS, not on conventional B-mode/Doppler diagnostic accuracy alone.

## Explicit Gaps Identified

- No confirmable, dedicated primary-source US-morphology series was found for: basal cell adenoma, lipoma, simple/benign cysts, salivary duct carcinoma, carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, or histotype-specific grayscale features of mucoepidermoid/adenoid cystic/acinic cell carcinoma.
- Author lists, exact volume/issue/page numbers, and/or PMIDs are missing for several otherwise-plausible citations (items 3, 7 partial, 10, 11, 12 cluster, 18, 19, 20 PMID, 21 PMID, 23 PMID, 24, 26, 27, 28) — these require direct primary-source confirmation (PubMed/journal site) before being used in the manuscript reference list.
- The Özçelik 2024 comparison study (#7) contains an internal numeric inconsistency across the retrieved search synthesis (US sensitivity reported as both 65% and 86% in the same result) that must be resolved against the primary article, not silently picked.
- Grayscale malignancy-sign literature shows genuine, reproducible **overlap between benign and malignant appearance**: well-defined margins are common even in malignant tumors in at least one series (#20, #22), and irregular shape was seen in a majority of both benign and malignant tumors in another dataset (#22) — this overlap should be stated plainly in the review rather than presenting grayscale signs as reliably discriminating.
- No 2023–2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of conventional (non-AI, non-contrast) B-mode/Doppler diagnostic accuracy for benign-vs-malignant salivary tumor differentiation was found; recent systematic-review activity clusters around AI/radiomics (#23) and CEUS (#17), which are in scope for the companion elastography/CEUS/MVI review.
- Sjögren-syndrome/OMERACT US scoring literature (#12) is real and multiply-indexed but individual PMID-to-citation pairing needs primary-source verification; also a scope question — may belong in an autoimmune-sialadenitis subsection rather than PART A/B as defined here.

Numeric findings (sensitivities, specificities, percentages) are inherently harder to cross-verify from snippets alone and are marked UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE wherever they appeared in only one search pass — these should be re-checked against the primary article before inclusion in the manuscript. The WebSearch budget for this session was exhausted before several planned follow-up queries (e.g., confirming basal cell adenoma imaging citation, REFCOR imaging recommendations, additional lymph-node US studies) could be run; these remain open gaps rather than resolved findings.
