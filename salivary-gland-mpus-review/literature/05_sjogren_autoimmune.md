## 1. OMERACT salivary gland ultrasound (SGUS) scoring system — defining/validation papers

**1.1** Jousse-Joulin S, Nowak E, Cornec D, Brown J, Carr A, Carotti M, Fisher B, Fradin J, Hocevar A, Jonsson MV, Luciano N, Milic V, Rout J, Theander E, Stel A, Bootsma H, Vissink A, Baldini C, Baer A, Ng WF, Bowman S, Alavi Z, Saraux A, Devauchelle-Pensec V.
"Salivary gland ultrasound abnormalities in primary Sjögren's syndrome: consensual US-SG core items definition and reliability."
*RMD Open*. 2017;3(1):e000364. DOI: 10.1136/rmdopen-2016-000364. PMCID: PMC5575597.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — consistent across PMC listing and multiple institutional repository mirrors (Johns Hopkins, Birmingham, Lund). PMID not independently confirmed (gap).
Note: this is the earlier OMERACT US working-group consensus paper defining core items and reliability, preceding the 2019/2022 scoring-system papers below.

**1.2** Jousse-Joulin S, D'Agostino MA, Nicolas C, et al.
"Video clip assessment of a salivary gland ultrasound scoring system in Sjögren's syndrome using consensual definitions: an OMERACT ultrasound working group reliability exercise."
*Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases*. 2019;78(7):967–973. DOI: 10.1136/annrheumdis-2019-215024. PMID: 31036626.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — title/PMID consistent across PubMed listing and search synthesis; this is the paper establishing the 2019 OMERACT 0–3 semiquantitative grey-scale SGUS score (parenchymal inhomogeneity focus; grades 0–1 normal, 2–3 pathological).

**1.3** Jousse-Joulin S, et al. (OMERACT Ultrasound subgroup on Sjögren's).
"Development of a new ultrasound scoring system to evaluate glandular inflammation in Sjögren's syndrome: an OMERACT reliability exercise."
*Rheumatology (Oxford)*. 2022;61(8):3341–3350. DOI: 10.1093/rheumatology/keab876. PMID: 34849616.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — consistent across Oxford Academic, PubMed, HAL open archive, and University of Copenhagen research portal. This paper extends the OMERACT work toward vascularization/inflammation scoring.

**1.4** Robin F, Albert JD, Lescoat A, Martel A, Perdriger A, DeBandt M, Maillot F, Coiffier G.
"Diagnostic Performances of Ultrasound Evaluation of Major Salivary Glands According to the 2019 Outcome Measures in Rheumatology Ultrasound Scoring System."
*Arthritis Care & Research (Hoboken)*. 2022. DOI: 10.1002/acr.24631. PMID: 33973395.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — consistent across Wiley/ACR journals, HAL preprint, PubMed listing. Reported performance: good sensitivity (~0.72) and excellent specificity (~0.91) for 2016 ACR/EULAR classification using a cut-off score >2 in at least one gland (per search-synthesis; exact volume/issue/pages not independently confirmed — gap).

**1.5** "Application of the OMERACT Grey-scale Ultrasound Scoring System for salivary glands in a single-centre cohort of patients with suspected Sjögren's syndrome." PMID: 33879581.
Status: **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE** — title and PMID seen only via PubMed listing snippet; journal name, full author list, year, volume/pages not corroborated by a second independent source. Explicit gap.

**1.6** "The agreement of the OMERACT grey-scale ultrasound scoring system for salivary glands and minor salivary gland biopsy results in a single-center cohort of patients with suspected Sjögren's disease."
*Skeletal Radiology*. 2025 (per Springer URL pattern 10.1007/s00256-025-04885-7). Also indexed as PMC12460492.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** for title/existence (Springer + PMC agree); authors, exact volume/pages, PMID **not confirmed** — gap. Reported: overall SGUS–MSGB (minor salivary gland biopsy) concordance ~67.2%; sensitivity of SGUS score ≥2 in ≥1 gland ~0.59, specificity ~0.75 (per search synthesis, unverified against primary text).

**1.7** "The Novel OMERACT Ultrasound Scoring System for Salivary Gland Changes in Patients With Sjögren Syndrome Is Associated With MRI and Salivary Flow Rates."
*The Journal of Rheumatology*. 2024;51(3):263– (exact end page not confirmed). DOI/PMID not confirmed.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** for title/journal/volume/issue (jrheum.org content page + early-view listing agree); exact page range, DOI, PMID = explicit gap.

**1.8** "The impact of an ultrasound atlas for scoring salivary glands in primary Sjögren's syndrome: a pilot study."
*Clinical Rheumatology*. 2023;42:3275–3281 (published online 2023-08-08). DOI: 10.1007/s10067-023-06696-4. PMID: 37553551.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — consistent across PMC, PubMed, University of Southern Denmark research portal. Finding: without atlas, inter-/intra-reader reliability were low; with a reference ultrasound atlas, inter-reader reliability improved to moderate (mean kappa 0.52; range 0.31–0.77) and intra-reader reliability to good (mean kappa 0.69; range 0.46–0.86).

**1.9** "Reliability Exercise of Ultrasound Salivary Glands in Sjögren's Disease: An International Web Training Initiative."
*Rheumatology and Therapy*. 2024;11(2):411–423. DOI: 10.1007/s40744-024-00645-6. PMID: 38372857.
Status: **VERIFIED (multi-source)** — consistent across Springer, HAL open archive, PubMed listing.
Note: a closely/identically titled item "Estimating the reliability of salivary gland ultrasound scoring in Sjögren's disease: the outcome of an international training workshop" appears indexed separately at Clinical and Experimental Rheumatology (clinexprheumatol.org, abstract ID 22520). ## 3. Interobserver / intraobserver reproducibility studies

Reproducibility data are embedded in several of the OMERACT papers above (1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.8, 1.9) plus additional studies:

- Consensual core-items paper (1.1, RMD Open 2017): established reliability of core US-SG lesion definitions among OMERACT-affiliated sonographers.
- Video-clip reliability exercise (1.2, Ann Rheum Dis 2019): OMERACT working-group reliability exercise using video clips with consensual definitions.
- OMERACT reliability exercise for new inflammation/vascularization score (1.3, Rheumatology 2022).
- Atlas pilot study (1.8, Clin Rheumatol 2023): quantifies improvement in kappa with a reference atlas (inter-reader kappa 0.31–0.77 range/mean 0.52 with atlas; intra-reader mean kappa 0.69).
- International web training initiative (1.9, Rheumatol Ther 2024): reliability among sonographers of varying experience via web-based training.
- "Ultrasound of the Major Salivary Glands is a Reliable Imaging Technique in Patients with Clinically Suspected Primary Sjögren's Syndrome." PMID: 29069669.
 Status: **VERIFIED existence via PubMed**; full citation (journal/authors/year/vol/pages) — gap.
- Additional reported figures from search synthesis (not independently verified against primary text, **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**): total-score intraobserver ICC 0.89–0.96; interobserver ICC 0.76–0.84; Cohen's kappa 0.60–0.83 depending on cut-off; a report attributed to "Salaffi et al." of interobserver kappa 0.88 (submandibular) and 0.93 (parotid) between two blinded radiologists — this Salaffi attribution could not be independently confirmed as a distinct citation (no full bibliographic record retrieved); treat as an unconfirmed claim pending primary-source check.
- ClinicalTrials.gov registration: "Evaluation of the Reproducibility Inter-observer of the Ultrasound Anomalies of the Salivary Glands in Sjögren's Disease," NCT02358213 — confirms a registered reproducibility study exists (likely underlying one of the Jousse-Joulin/OMERACT reliability papers above); registry entry only, not a publication citation.

**Overall reproducibility conclusion supported by literature found**: reliability of the OMERACT grey-scale SGUS score is generally good-to-excellent for experienced/trained readers under exercise conditions (video clips, workshops), and demonstrably improves further with structured reference materials (atlas) or web-based training — but reliability among less experienced readers or in unstructured "real-world" settings is more modest (moderate kappa), and reliability specifically for echogenicity/homogeneity sub-items is lower than for overall gestalt scoring. This nuance (excellent-under-training vs moderate-in-practice) should be represented honestly in the review rather than citing a single reliability figure as universal.

## 5. Sarcoidosis — salivary gland US features

Literature found is sparse and largely case-report/review level; **no validated sarcoidosis-specific SGUS scoring or large cohort study was identified in this search**.

**5.1** "Sarcoidosis in the Head and Neck: An Illustrative Review of Clinical Presentations and Imaging Findings." *American Journal of Roentgenology (AJR)*.
Status: **VERIFIED existence (AJR journal site)**; full citation (authors/year/volume/pages/DOI/PMID) not confirmed — gap. General head-and-neck imaging review, not sonography-specific.

**5.2** "A rare case of sarcoidosis involving primarily the parotid glands: US and MRI correlation." Eurorad case report (eurorad.org/case/14570).
Status: **UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE**, case-report level only (Eurorad teaching-case database, not a peer-reviewed indexed journal article in the conventional sense).
Reported US features (per case report, single source, not corroborated elsewhere): hypoechoic parotid nodules, some with microcalcifications, increased parenchymal blood flow; submandibular glands with increased vascularity and heterogeneous echotexture.

**Explicit gap**: no OMERACT-type or otherwise validated scoring system for salivary gland sarcoidosis was found. Imaging descriptions available are nonspecific (overlap with chronic sialadenitis, TB, other granulomatous disease, and Sjögren's) and literature is essentially confined to case reports/small series and general head-and-neck sarcoidosis reviews. This should be stated as a genuine evidence gap in the review, not glossed over.

## 7. Summary of explicit gaps identified

1. Exact DOI/PMID/volume-page data missing for several OMERACT-family papers (1.5, 1.6, 1.7) despite confirmed existence/title — recommend direct PubMed/journal verification before manuscript submission.
2. Possible duplicate/companion publication ambiguity between the Rheumatology and Therapy 2024 web-training paper and a similarly titled Clinical and Experimental Rheumatology abstract (1.9) — needs resolution.
3. SGUS–ESSDAI correlation strength is inconsistent across studies found (from strong correlation in one cohort to no significant association in another) — this heterogeneity should be represented, not averaged into one number.
4. A specific "Salaffi et al." interobserver kappa figure (0.88 parotid / 0.93 submandibular) surfaced only in AI-generated search synthesis with no independently retrievable bibliographic record — do not cite until confirmed against primary text.
5. The 2024 EULAR management recommendations reference (Sec 2.10) needs full independent citation verification (authors, exact title, volume/pages, DOI, PMID) before use.
6. Sarcoidosis: no validated SGUS scoring system or substantial cohort literature found — evidence base is essentially case-report level. State as a genuine gap in the review.
7. HIV-related salivary disease: literature is largely older (pre-2010, several pre-2000) and could not be as thoroughly cross-verified in this session (WebSearch budget exhausted); most entries are UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full bibliographic detail. No modern (post-HAART-era) large systematic US cohort study was identified — flagged as a gap, and as an area needing dedicated re-search in a future session with fresh search budget and/or working direct fetch access to PubMed/AJR.
8. IgG4-RD: possible non-identity or unclear relationship between references 4.2 and 4.3 (case report vs. what may be the original Japanese-language study it reviews) needs resolution before both are cited independently.
9. General limitation: because direct WebFetch/curl access to PubMed, PMC, Springer, Wiley, ScienceDirect, Taylor & Francis, MDPI, Oxford Academic, BMJ, Google, DOI.org, and HAL was blocked at the network egress layer throughout this task, **no reference in this file has been confirmed against full primary-source text** — all verification is at the level of cross-matching search-result titles/DOIs/PMIDs across independent indexing domains. Before submission, every reference here should be re-verified against the actual publication (abstract at minimum) once direct access is available.
