## 3. Technique-specific findings (frequency, positioning, compression, harmonic imaging, bilateral comparison)

- **Transducer frequency:** consistently reported across sources as high-frequency linear array, commonly **7–14 MHz** (Ultrasoundpaedia-style summary) or **5–13 MHz** (Johnson et al. 2025, Ultraschall Med) or **5–14 MHz** (Hoffman et al. 2024 standardized-analysis protocol). A lower-frequency curved probe (5–7 MHz) is recommended as an adjunct to reach the deep parotid lobe / deep submandibular gland in some sources. **No single frequency range is universally standardized across sources — ranges cited differ slightly (5–13, 5–14, 7–14 MHz) depending on author/era.**
- **Bilateral comparison:** explicitly recommended by Johnson et al. 2025 ("should always start on the non-pathological side") — i.e., contralateral/bilateral comparison is a stated technique principle in at least one recent, well-verified CME review.
- **Harmonic imaging:** found only generic technical description (tissue harmonic imaging principles — reduces reverberation/side-lobe artifact, improves SNR and resolution in larger body habitus) plus one general statement that "new technologies including high-resolution probes and harmonic imaging" help delineate salivary lesion features. **No salivary-gland-specific study directly comparing harmonic vs fundamental imaging was found** — GAP.
- **Compression technique:** **no salivary-gland-specific literature on compression technique was found** in this search — GAP (compression is a known general US technique for other organs/vascular structures but no salivary-specific reference located).
- **Patient positioning:** not found as a discretely referenced/standardized item beyond generic "supine, neck extended" type descriptions implied in general head & neck US texts; no dedicated methodological reference located — GAP.
- **Landmark anatomy for facial nerve (not directly visible on US):**
  - Retromandibular vein used as the standard surrogate landmark separating the superficial and deep parotid lobes, because it runs directly superficial/lateral to the facial nerve trunk — consistently repeated across multiple sources (EPOS ECR poster "The Parotid Gland On Ultrasound: Old But Gold"; Journal of Clinical Imaging Science practical guide; ScienceDirect article on Stensen's-duct-based facial nerve localization).
  - A dedicated technique paper was found: **"Ultrasonographic Localization of Parotid Gland Tumor Relative to the Facial Nerve Using Stensen's Duct Criterion"** (Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, via ScienceDirect) — full author list/year/DOI not retrieved (WebFetch blocked) — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, worth a direct follow-up citation check.
  - Anatomical variant case reports on retromandibular vein position relative to facial nerve (e.g., "An Unusual Position of Retromandibular Vein in Relation to Facial Nerve," "A Rare Case of a Fenestrated Retromandibular Vein Lying Lateral to the Facial Nerve Bifurcation," both PMC-hosted case reports) — exist per search snippets but not independently opened; useful as anatomical-variant citations but flagged UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE pending direct read.
- **Vascular landmarks:**
  - External carotid artery, facial artery (4th branch of ECA), submental artery — course posterolateral/deep to submandibular gland at the angle of mandible; identifiable on grey-scale and colour Doppler. Consistent across multiple general anatomy/US sources (StatPearls, Iowa Head & Neck Protocols, Radiology Key, Kenhub) though these are largely non-peer-reviewed educational/reference sites, not primary literature — note lower evidentiary weight.
  - Superficial temporal artery and retromandibular vein cited as parotid landmarks together with masseter, mandible, posterior belly of digastric, SCM, styloid process, internal jugular vein (from Ultrasoundpaedia synthesis — an educational radiology teaching site, not peer-reviewed; treat as orientation-only, re-verify against a peer-reviewed anatomy source before citing).

## 4. Normal dimensions, echogenicity, and duct diameters

### 4.1 Gland size — classic biometry study
- **Author:** P. Dost
- **Title:** "Ultrasonographic biometry in normal salivary glands"
- **Journal:** European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology
- **Year/vol/pages:** 1997; 254 Suppl 1:S18–19
- **Findings reported in snippets:** n=50 disease-free subjects. Submandibular gland: AP length 35 ± 5.7 mm; paramandibular depth 14.3 ± 5.7 mm (NB: one snippet gave "± 2.9 mm" for this figure and another gave "± 5.7 mm" — **internal inconsistency across search-snippet paraphrases, needs primary-source check**); frontal-scan extension 33.7 ± 5.4 mm. Parotid gland: 46.3 ± 7.7 mm (axis parallel to mandibular ramus) × 37.4 ± 5.6 mm (transverse axis); 7.4 ± 1.7 mm lateral to mandible; 22.8 ± 3.6 mm dorsal to mandible.
- **Status:** VERIFIED as to existence/journal/year (PubMed listing + SpringerLink page agree), but the **numeric values themselves are UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE-PARAPHRASE and internally inconsistent between two search-engine syntheses** — the actual abstract/full text must be read directly before any number from this paper is used in the review. Treat as a real, classic, citable reference for "biometric normal ranges exist but are old/single-center/small-n," not as a source of verbatim numbers yet.

### 4.2 Echogenicity — large modern cohort
- **Authors:** Choi I, Na DG, Paik W
- **Title:** "Ultrasonographic echogenicity of normal salivary glands in adults: comparison of submandibular and parotid glands"
- **Journal:** Ultrasonography (formerly known as J Clin Ultrasound / Korean journal "Ultrasonography," e-ultrasonography.org)
- **Year/vol/issue:** 2021; 40(3) (PMC8217796)
- **Findings reported:** n=969 consecutive adults with normal glands, stratified 19–29y (n=27), 30–49y (n=273), ≥50y (n=669). Submandibular echogenicity relative to parotid: similar in 73.0%, homogeneously hypoechoic in 27.0%. Hypoechogenicity of SMG decreased with age: 59.3% (19–29y) → 36.3% (30–49y) → 22.1% (≥50y) — i.e., **age is a documented confound on submandibular gland echogenicity relative to parotid**, directly supporting point 6 of the assignment (factors influencing echogenicity).
- **Status:** VERIFIED (multiple independent sources — PMC listing, e-ultrasonography.org journal page, and the search-engine synthesis — agree on authors, journal, year/volume, and the age-stratified percentages appear consistently across the two independent search passes that surfaced them). This is a strong, modern, appropriately-powered reference for the review's discussion of lack of a single "normal echogenicity" and age as a modifier.

### 4.3 BMI / metabolic factors
- **Title (from snippet):** "Multimodal ultrasound investigation (grey scale, Doppler and 2D-SWE) of salivary and lacrimal glands in healthy people and patients with diabetes mellitus and/or obesity, with or without sialosis"
- **Source:** PubMed listing, PMID 31476205 (not independently opened — WebFetch blocked)
- **Findings reported:** no significant elasticity-modulus variation by BMI/sex/age in this cohort; but echogenicity, homogeneity, posterior-border definition, and glandular area differed between healthy controls and diabetic/obese/sialosis patients.
- **Status:** UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (only one search snippet surfaced this; authors/journal/year/volume not retrieved). Directly relevant to "factors influencing gland size/echogenicity" — follow up with a direct PubMed/journal check for full citation before use.
- **GAP:** No dedicated, well-verified study specifically isolating **hydration status** as a variable affecting salivary gland US appearance was found despite a targeted search — explicit gap.

### 4.4 Explicit statements on lack of standardized normal ranges
- Multiple independent search snippets converge on the statement that **"specifications about the size of healthy salivary glands are not available to date"** and that shear-wave elastography quantification of salivary parenchyma "is compromised by lack of a standardized approach" — these paraphrases derive from the Hoffman et al. 2024 standardization proposal (§1.6) and related elastography-standardization commentary. This directly supports assignment point 6 (explicit literature acknowledgment that no universally accepted normal size range exists) and should be cited via Hoffman 2024 (verified full citation above) — do not cite the "no standardized range" claim to a source whose full citation is unconfirmed.

### 4.5 Duct diameters — Stensen's and Wharton's ducts
- **Key caveat established by this search: normative duct-diameter data found were derived from SIALOGRAPHY (contrast fluoroscopy) and cadaveric/histologic morphometry, NOT from ultrasound.** On US, both ducts are normally **not visualized unless dilated** (a consistent, multiply-repeated statement across sources, e.g., Ento Key "Salivary Gland Imaging," EPOS sialography posters) — sialagogue provocation (e.g., ascorbic acid) can transiently dilate Stensen's duct to render it visible.
- Sialographic/morphometric values found (attribute to be confirmed — likely Zenk J et al., not independently confirmed as author by this search; UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for authorship):
  - Stensen's duct: mean diameter 0.5–1.4 mm across 4 measured points (older reference, cited via PubMed title "[The diameter of the Stenon and Wharton ducts. Significance for diagnosis and therapy]," PMID 10023592, German-language, full citation not retrieved); a separate/more recent sialography-based study (Wenzel PA et al., "Stensen's Duct Dynamic Anatomy Assessed with Sialography," SAGE journal, 2024) gives narrowest width 0.8 mm (IQR 0.7–1.15) and widest 2.0 mm (IQR 1.4–2.2).
  - Wharton's duct: mean diameter 0.5–1.5 mm (older reference, same PMID 10023592 source) vs. proximal/mid/distal segment widths 1.8/1.1/1.6 mm (source attribution uncertain from search synthesis — needs primary check).
- **Status: UNVERIFIED / GAP.** No ultrasound-specific normative duct-diameter dataset was found. The review will need to state explicitly that normal US duct calibre reference values are not established in the same way sialographic values are, and that visualization itself is the key normal finding (non-visualization = normal), rather than a measured diameter range. The Wenzel et al. 2024 Journal (SAGE, likely *Craniomaxillofacial Trauma & Reconstruction* or similar — journal name not confirmed) reference is worth a direct follow-up for full citation.

## 5. Anatomical variants

- **Accessory parotid gland (APG):** well-supported by multiple sources (IMAIOS e-Anatomy, PMC5600370 on CT, EPOS ECR poster). Prevalence figures varied substantially between sources (21–69% in cadaveric series per one synthesis vs. ~20% population prevalence vs. 10.2% on CT per another) — **these are inconsistent across snippets and must be reconciled against the primary CT paper (PMC5600370, "The accessory parotid gland and facial process of the parotid gland on computed tomography") before citing a specific prevalence figure.** Located ~0.6–1.05 cm anterior to the main gland, superficial to masseter, closely related to buccal branches of facial nerve and transverse facial artery; on US, best seen with high-frequency (10–15 MHz) transducer, extending across the masseter muscle. Clinically important because tumours can arise here and be missed if the accessory gland is not specifically sought.
- **Accessory submandibular ("submaxillary") gland:** one source located — "Accessory submaxillary gland: Two new case reports and a literature review" (PMC7511051) — not independently opened; UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citation not retrieved.
- **Retromandibular vein variants relative to facial nerve:** two case-report-level sources found (PMC6247713 "unusual position," PMC10800706 "fenestrated" vein lateral to facial nerve bifurcation) — relevant to the "landmarks may be unreliable in a minority of patients" caveat for the review, but both UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (titles only, not opened).
- **Kuttner (Küttner) node as a normal landmark node vs. Küttner tumour (pathology) — IMPORTANT AMBIGUITY FLAGGED:** one educational source (Ultrasoundpaedia) refers to a "Kuttner lymph node" as a normal, prominent, expected lymph node located between the parotid tail and submandibular gland. This appears to be informal/teaching terminology and should **not** be conflated with "Küttner tumour" (chronic sclerosing sialadenitis), which is a distinct pathological entity abundantly documented in the literature (multiple sources on sonographic appearance of Küttner tumour were found, e.g., Ultrasound in Medicine & Biology, Korean case series). **This "Kuttner node" naming as applied to a normal lymph node could not be corroborated in any peer-reviewed source in this search — treat as unverified/likely informal terminology and either drop it or independently confirm before use.**
- **Sublingual gland — visualization limits as a "variant" of visibility rather than true anatomical variant:** consistently described as small, deep, often incompletely visualized, with 8–20 small excretory ducts superiorly — makes complete normal-anatomy depiction inherently variable/operator-and-patient-dependent. Sources: general sonoanatomy summaries (EPOS posters, teaching sites) — largely non-peer-reviewed; a peer-reviewed anchor reference for this specific claim was not found — GAP.
- **Minor salivary glands:** visible/assessable by US only when a threshold of ≥4 glands and ≥8 mm² surface area is met (from a study on minor salivary gland US vs. histopathology in Sjögren's/sicca syndrome — PMC12568837, "Agreement between glandular ultrasonography and histopathology of minor salivary glands in adults with sicca syndrome") — full citation (authors/journal/year) not retrieved; UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE. This is a disease-context (labial biopsy comparison) study rather than a normal-anatomy paper, so of secondary relevance but useful for feasibility statement.
