## 1. US-guided FNAC of salivary gland lesions

**Eom HJ, Lee JH, Ko MS, Choi YJ, Yoon RG, Cho KJ, Nam SY, Baek JH.** "Comparison of fine-needle aspiration and core needle biopsy under ultrasonographic guidance for detecting malignancy and for the tissue-specific diagnosis of salivary gland tumors." *AJNR Am J Neuroradiol.* 2015 Jun;36(6):1188-93. DOI: 10.3174/ajnr.A4247. PMID: 25678480.

## 2. The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (MSRSGC)

**Founding reference (1st edition, atlas/book):** Faquin WC, Rossi ED (eds). *The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology.* Springer, published February 2018 (1st edition). International effort sponsored by the American Society of Cytopathology (ASC) and the International Academy of Cytology (IAC); defines a six-tiered diagnostic category system (Non-diagnostic; Non-neoplastic; Atypia of Undetermined Significance; Neoplasm: Benign; Salivary Gland Neoplasm of Uncertain Malignant Potential [SUMP]; Suspicious for Malignancy; Malignant — six numbered categories in total). — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement across Springer book listing, AAO-HNS Bulletin article, multiple institutional validation papers).

**Faquin WC.** "Refining the Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology" [editorial]. *Cancer Cytopathol.* 2021;129:22-23. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (search-derived citation; DOI/PMID not independently confirmed via WebFetch; volume/page numbers as reported by search synthesis). Content per source: risk of malignancy is low (<5%) for pleomorphic adenoma classified as "Neoplasm: Benign" or SUMP; oncocytoid lesions with differential diagnosis of Warthin tumor classified as SUMP carry elevated risk of malignancy (>15%) in some series.

**Rossi ED, Baloch Z, Barkan G, et al.** "Second edition of the Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology: Refining the role of salivary gland FNA." Simultaneously co-published (identical content, minor stylistic differences) in three journals per publisher notice:
- *J Am Soc Cytopathol.* 2024;13(1):67-77.
- *Cancer Cytopathol.* 2024;132(1):10-21.
- *Cytopathology.* 2024. DOI: 10.1111/cyt.13331.
— VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: Wiley listings for both Cytopathology and Cancer Cytopathology versions, CAP TODAY commentary, ScienceDirect/JASC listing, ResearchGate, all describe the same simultaneous tri-journal publication with matching title/author-lead). PMID for the Cancer Cytopathology or JASC version not independently confirmed via WebFetch (search budget exhausted before further confirmation).
Content: published July 2023 (online-first)/2024 (print); includes refined risk-of-malignancy (ROM) estimates derived from systematic reviews/meta-analyses accumulated since 2018, a new imaging chapter, updated ancillary/molecular testing guidance, and nomenclature refinements. Retains the six-tier framework of the 1st edition.

**Validation/risk-of-malignancy studies by category** (institutional, not the founding MSRSGC papers themselves):
- Single-center study of 2156 cases (title: "The Milan system for reporting salivary gland cytopathology—A single-center study of 2156 cases," PMC10278457) reporting ROM determined in 753 cases with histopathologic follow-up. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, authors/journal/year not fully retrieved.
- Another institutional study reported ROM by category as: Category I (non-diagnostic) 37.5%; II (non-neoplastic) 9.0%; III (atypia of undetermined significance) 50.0%; IVa (benign neoplasm) 4.7%; IVb (SUMP) 50.0%; V (suspicious for malignancy) 100.0%; VI (malignant) 71.0%. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (source paper title/authors not confirmed; these are illustrative of the wide inter-institutional variability in ROM, especially for lower-volume categories such as AUS and SUMP).
- A separate institution's series reported: non-diagnostic 40%, non-neoplastic 25%, AUS 0%, benign neoplasm 1%, SUMP 54.54%, suspicious for malignancy 90.9%, malignant 100%. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE.
- "Utility of the Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology in Parotid Gland Masses: The Experience of Two Tertiary Centers" (PMC10746347) and "Effectiveness of The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytology: A 5-Year Retrospective Review" (PMC11887628) — both UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citations not retrieved; cited by search engine as additional validation cohorts.
- "A 4-Year Retrospective Analysis of Salivary Gland Cytopathology Using the Milan System... and Ancillary Studies" (PMC6966685) — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE.

**Interpretation for the review:** The Milan System's ROM figures are consistently reported by the founding group as evidence-derived (systematic review/meta-analysis-based in the 2nd edition), but institutional validation cohorts show substantial ROM heterogeneity, particularly for Category III (AUS) and Category IVb (SUMP) — this should be stated as a known limitation/controversy of the system in the review, not smoothed over.

**No 2025-2026 update papers to MSRSGC were identified.** The 2nd edition (2023/2024) is the most recent version found. This is a gap to flag explicitly if the review claims "2023-2026 updates" — only the 2023/2024 2nd edition was located; no further update after that has been found.

## 3. US-guided core needle biopsy (CNB) of salivary gland lesions

**"Ultrasound-guided core biopsy in the diagnosis of parotid neoplasia: an overview and update with a review of the literature."** *Br J Radiol.* 2022;95(1130):20210972. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (authors not retrieved from snippet; journal/year/volume/article number confirmed via Oxford Academic listing found in search, but WebFetch to read full text was blocked).

**"Ultrasonography-Guided Core-Needle Biopsy of Parotid Gland Masses."** PMC7976420. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citation not retrieved.

**"Diagnostic Accuracy and Post-Procedural Complications Associated with Ultrasound-Guided Core Needle Biopsy in the Preoperative Evaluation of Parotid Tumors."** PMC9424419. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citation not retrieved.

**Technique summary** (synthesized across multiple search-engine snippets, not independently verified against a single source): 18–20 gauge needle with automated/semi-automated biopsy device; posterior-inferior approach from the tail of the superficial parotid lobe to avoid the facial nerve; needle angle to skin kept <45° to avoid the parotid duct and facial nerve; real-time color Doppler used to trace intraparotid vessels/duct and avoid vascular injury. — Treat as a general technique description aggregated from several reviews, not a single verified citation.

**Complications** (aggregated, not single-source): reported as minimal overall (~0.5% adverse events in one aggregate description), predominantly hematoma; occasional transient facial weakness attributed to local anesthetic infiltration rather than nerve transection; no confirmed reports of permanent facial nerve injury or tumor seeding from US-guided CNB found in the retrieved snippets. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE / search-aggregate; specific source studies for these numbers not individually confirmed.

**CNB for Sjögren's syndrome (non-oncologic indication, included for completeness given "salivary gland lesions" scope may extend to diagnostic biopsy for autoimmune disease):**
- "Ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy and incisional biopsy of the parotid gland are comparable in diagnosis of primary Sjögren's syndrome." *Rheumatology (Oxford).* 2023;62(8):2765-[pages incomplete]. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (journal/year/volume/first page found; full page range, authors, DOI, PMID not retrieved).
- ACR Meeting Abstract: "Ultrasound-Guided Core Needle Biopsy of the Major Salivary Glands Is a Safe and Useful Diagnostic Tool in the Evaluation of Suspected or Established Sjögren's Syndrome." — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, conference abstract only (not peer-reviewed full paper), authors/year not retrieved.

**Gap noted:** Needle-gauge-stratified complication/yield data (e.g., 18G vs 20G vs 14G) were not found broken out in retrievable snippets; the literature appears to converge on 18-20G devices but a head-to-head gauge comparison study specific to salivary gland was not identified.

## 4. FNA vs CNB — direct comparative studies

**Eom HJ et al. AJNR 2015;36(6):1188-93.** (Full citation and numbers under Section 1/3 above — this is the most consistently cross-referenced direct comparison.) VERIFIED (multi-source agreement).

**Cho HW, [et al.]** "Comparison of core needle biopsy and fine-needle aspiration in diagnosis of malignant salivary gland neoplasm: Systematic review and meta-analysis." *Head Neck.* 2020. PMID: 32671867 (per PubMed listing found in search results; DOI/volume/pages not independently retrieved via WebFetch). — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full bibliographic detail beyond title/journal/year/PMID (these fields were consistently shown across two independent search result listings — Wiley "Head & Neck" abstract and PubMed — so title/journal/year/PMID can be treated as VERIFIED by multi-source agreement; numeric results not retrieved).

**Kassem K, Safia A, Abd Elhadi U, Merchavy S.** "Fine-needle aspiration or core needle biopsy? A meta-analysis of diagnostic accuracy and procedural outcomes in salivary gland tumors." *Eur J Radiol.* Vol. 194, 2026 (published online 17 Nov 2025). DOI: 10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112532. PMID: 41264980. Affiliation: Rebecca Ziv Medical Center, Safed, Israel. — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: PubMed listing, ScienceDirect listing, EJR journal site listing all concordant on title/authors/journal).
Reported numbers: 15 studies, 3,669 patients, 4,000 biopsies; literature search conducted across PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar on 8 April 2025. CNB sensitivity ≈99% vs FNA ≈68%; specificity reported as similarly high for both modalities (exact specificity percentages not retrieved in snippet). FNA had significantly higher non-diagnostic and repeat-procedure rates than CNB. CNB described as safe with low complication rates.
**Note:** this is the most recent (2025/2026) and largest-scale comparative meta-analysis identified for this domain — recommend as anchor reference for the FNA-vs-CNB section, but numeric details (specificity, exact non-diagnostic rates, complication rates) should be re-verified against the full text before quoting precise figures, since only search-snippet-level data was obtainable here.

**"Comparison of ultrasound-guided core biopsy versus fine-needle aspiration biopsy in the evaluation of salivary gland lesions."** PubMed PMID: 23109044. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (title and PMID found; authors, journal, year, volume/pages, numeric results not retrieved).

**"Role of ultrasound-guided core-needle biopsy in the assessment of head and neck lesions: a meta-analysis and systematic review of the literature."** Listed in NCBI Bookshelf DARE (Database of Abstracts of Reviews of Effects), NBK127351. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE; broader head-and-neck scope, not salivary-gland-specific; full citation not retrieved.

**Accuracy of Core Needle Biopsy Versus Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology for Diagnosing Salivary Gland Tumors.** *J Pathol Transl Med* (jpatholtm.org), DOI prefix 10.4132/jptm.2015.01.03. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (journal and DOI found; authors, exact year/volume/pages/PMID and numeric results not retrieved; full text access blocked).

**Synthesis across comparative studies:** All identified comparative studies concur in direction of effect — CNB shows higher sensitivity/accuracy, particularly for malignant tumor detection and tissue-specific (histotype) subtyping, and lower non-diagnostic/repeat-procedure rates than FNA, at comparable/low complication rates. This directional consistency across ≥3 independent studies (Eom 2015, Cho 2020, Kassem 2025/2026) is treated as VERIFIED at the level of "direction of effect"; exact numeric magnitude varies by study and should be reported as ranges with individual citations, not pooled without the original meta-analytic weighting.

## 5. Role of rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE)

**Finding: no dedicated, well-specified peer-reviewed study on ROSE specifically for US-guided salivary gland FNA was retrieved with a confirmable full citation.** Search results returned only general/adjacent material:
- General statements that ROSE is "an important consideration in salivary gland biopsy decision-making" and that FNA diagnostic validity depends on cytopathologist experience — these are search-engine syntheses, not a specific dated citation.
- Adjacent literature on ROSE was found for other organ sites (e.g., EUS-FNB vs FNA with on-site cytopathology for pancreatic masses — not salivary gland).

**This is an explicit, notable gap**: despite ROSE being a plausible and commonly discussed adjunct in salivary gland cytology practice, no specific comparative study (ROSE vs no-ROSE adequacy/non-diagnostic rates for salivary gland FNA) was located via the searches performed in this session. The review should either (a) state this as a genuine literature gap, or (b) a further targeted search (PubMed native search, not available in this session) is recommended specifically for terms like "rapid on-site evaluation" + "salivary gland" + "adequacy."

## 6. Color Doppler / CEUS in guiding biopsy target selection

**No salivary-gland-specific study directly demonstrating CEUS- or Doppler-guided biopsy target selection (i.e., a dedicated interventional CEUS-guided-biopsy trial for salivary gland lesions) was retrieved.** Found instead:
- General/extrapolated statements that "CEUS enables real-time visualization of viable tumor regions, facilitating accurate needle placement and minimizing sampling from necrotic or hemorrhagic areas" — this statement in the search synthesis appears to generalize from CEUS-guided biopsy literature in **other organs** (e.g., "Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasound Improves the Pathological Outcomes of US-Guided Core Needle Biopsy That Targets the Viable Area of Anterior Mediastinal Masses," PMC5822857 — mediastinal, not salivary gland) and should NOT be cited as salivary-gland-specific evidence without a direct source.
- **"Contrast-enhanced ultrasound in the evaluation of parotid gland lesions: an update of the literature."** PMC4951786. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citation not retrieved; addresses CEUS for lesion characterization/differentiation (e.g., pleomorphic adenoma vs Warthin tumor), not specifically biopsy-target guidance.
- **"Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasound in the Differentiation between the Most Common Benign Parotid Gland Tumors: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis."** PMID: 36555976. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full detail (title/PMID found via two independent listings — PMC and PubMed — so title/PMID treated as VERIFIED by multi-source agreement; this paper is about diagnostic differentiation, not interventional guidance).
- **Wakonig et al.** "Multiparametric Ultrasound for Preoperative Assessment of Parotid Tumors: A Novel Diagnostic Pathway." *Head Neck.* 2026 (Wiley Online Library, DOI 10.1002/hed.70036). — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full detail; relevant as a very recent (2026) MPUS pathway paper referencing CEUS/SWE combined with B-mode, and citing ASCO/ESMO/German AWMF guideline alignment for US-guided biopsy confirmation of malignancy, but does not appear (from the snippet) to be specifically about CEUS-guided needle targeting/necrosis avoidance during biopsy itself.

**Explicit gap:** Direct evidence for CEUS or color/power Doppler being used prospectively to select the biopsy needle path (vessel avoidance, viable-tissue targeting) specifically within a salivary gland lesion — as opposed to general lesion characterization — was not found as a dedicated study in this session's searches. The review should state this as an evidence gap / extrapolation from other organ systems (breast, liver, mediastinum, lung) rather than cite salivary-gland-specific trial data that does not appear to exist yet.

## 7. US-guided drainage of abscesses/collections; US-guided cyst treatment

Reported: comparative study of needle aspiration vs surgical incision for parotid abscess; needle aspiration required on average 1.88 procedures vs 1.10 for surgical intervention (i.e., needle aspiration more often required repeat procedures). ## 8. Minimally invasive thermal/chemical ablation of salivary gland lesions

Evidence stratified explicitly by maturity, per task instructions.

### 8a. Established / most substantial evidence base (multiple studies, including a prospective safety-feasibility trial and a systematic review) — still overall "emerging/limited" by oncologic-therapy standards, but the most-studied ablation modality for salivary gland tumors

**Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) — for Warthin tumor and pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid, all indications benign only:**

- **[Author names not fully retrieved].** "A Safety and Feasibility Trial of Ultrasound-Guided Radiofrequency Ablation of Parotid Warthin's Tumor." Journal per search: *OTO Open* / AAO-HNSF journals (DOI 10.1002/ohn.417). PMID: 37435621. — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: PubMed listing, Wiley/AAO-HNSF listing, and a scite.ai listing all concordant on title/PMID/DOI). Reported: volume reduction 7.48 mL (68.4%) from baseline at a median 45 weeks post-procedure; complications: 3 patients with transient facial nerve paresis (1 resolved within hours, 2 by 12 weeks), 3 patients with great auricular nerve numbness; no significant difference vs a historical parotidectomy cohort in facial nerve paresis/other minor complication rates. This is a prospective trial (highest evidence level identified for any ablation modality in this domain).

- **Do K, Kawana E, Tian S, Bigcas JL.** "Treatment of Warthin's Tumors of the Parotid Gland With Radiofrequency Ablation: A Systematic Review of the Current Literature." SAGE journal, DOI 10.1177/01455613241248119 (2024; journal identity consistent with *Ear, Nose & Throat Journal* based on DOI prefix, not independently confirmed). — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full bibliographic detail (title/authors/DOI found via one search listing; volume/pages/PMID and pooled numeric results not retrieved due to search budget exhaustion before a confirming second query could be run).

- **Lee CY, Lin WC, Luo SD, Chiang PL, Lin AN, Wang CK, Chao CY.** "Safety and Efficacy of Radiofrequency Ablation for Superficial Parotid Pleomorphic Adenoma." *Korean J Radiol.* 2025 May;26(5):460-470. DOI: 10.3348/kjr.2024.0909. PMID: 40307200. — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: kjronline.org listing and PubMed-derived listing concordant on all bibliographic fields). Numeric efficacy/complication results not retrieved beyond title (full text access blocked); should be re-pulled for the actual review draft.

### 8b. Limited evidence — small case series / single/two-center retrospective studies, benign parotid tumors only

**Microwave ablation (MWA):**

- **Teng [et al.]** "Microwave ablation of benign parotid gland tumors under ultrasound guidance: a multicenter retrospective case series." *Gland Surgery* (gs.amegroups.org), also indexed as PMC12596457. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full citation (year/volume/pages/DOI/PMID not retrieved; full text blocked). Reported: 13 consecutive patients (July 2013–June 2018 per snippet — note this date range predates the retrospective "multicenter" framing and should be re-checked against full text for accuracy), 12 of 13 biopsied tumors were benign (6 pleomorphic adenoma, 4 Warthin tumor, 1 dermoid cyst, 1 lymphoid nodular hyperplasia) treated with MWA; age range 10-79 years (median 60.5); tumor diameter 17.8-49 mm (median 26.15 mm); ablation time 70-598 sec (median 155 sec); complete absorption at 8-37 months (median 23.5 months); no residual tumor or serious complications reported.

- **[Case report].** "Ultrasound-guided percutaneous microwave ablation of parotid gland adenolymphoma: A case report." *Medicine (Baltimore).* 2019 (per journals.lww.com URL pattern "2019/08300"). PMID: 31464905. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE (title/PMID/approximate year found; authors, exact volume/issue/pages/DOI not retrieved). Single case report — lowest evidence tier.

**Combined MWA + RFA, two-center retrospective (mixed thermal ablation):**

- "Ultrasound-guided percutaneous thermal ablation of parotid tumors: experience from two-centers." *Int J Hyperthermia.* 2023 (published ahead of 2024 print), DOI: 10.1080/02656736.2023.2290924. — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for authors/exact volume-issue-pages/PMID (journal, year, and DOI found via two independent T&F listings, so treated as VERIFIED for those fields by multi-source agreement; numeric results below from a single search snippet, so treated as UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for numbers). Reported: 23 patients (13 male, 10 female; median age 65, range 5-91) treated with MWA or RFA Jan 2020-Mar 2023; mean volume reduction rate 37.03%±10.23% (1 month), 56.52%±8.76% (3 months), 82.28%±7.89% (6 months), 89.39%±6.45% (12 months); mean cosmetic score improved from 3.39±0.66 to 1.75±0.93 (p<0.001).

**Ethanol sclerotherapy (chemical ablation), Warthin tumor and parotid cysts:**

- "Ultrasound-guided ethanol sclerotherapy for non-surgical treatment of Warthin's tumor." PubMed PMID: 33130530 (2020, per ScienceDirect listing). — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE for full detail (title/PMID/approx. year found; authors, journal, volume/pages, DOI not retrieved).
- Lee E, Badger C, Mamidi IS, Benito DA, Li L, Goodman JF, Thakkar PG, Joshi A. "Ultrasound-guided ethanol sclerotherapy for multifocal unilateral Warthin's tumor after partial parotidectomy." SAGE journal (journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1742271X211030302), 2022; also indexed PMC9058389. — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: SAGE listing and PMC listing concordant on title, authors, year, DOI pattern). Single case report — low evidence tier despite full author list being available.
- Ongoing/registered clinical trial: NCT05221736, "Ethanol and Ultrasound-Guided Ethanol Sclerotherapy (UGES) in Parotid Neoplasms and Warthin's Tumor of Parotid Gland." — VERIFIED as a registered trial (ClinicalTrials.gov registry mirror found), but this is a trial registration, not yet a completed/published outcomes study — flag as ongoing/registered, evidence not yet mature.
- General statement (search synthesis, not single-paper-attributable): UGES has documented efficacy for benign thyroid nodules and branchial cleft cysts, and has been extended to parotid cysts and Warthin tumor — extrapolation across a "Current Trends and Controversies in the Management of Warthin Tumor of the Parotid Gland" review (MDPI, mdpi.com/2075-4418/11/8/1467) — UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE, full citation not retrieved.

**Salivary gland (chemical) ablation for sialorrhea — different indication (functional/hypersecretion control, not tumor):**
- **Begley KA, Braswell LE, Noritz GH, Murakami JW.** "Salivary gland ablation: introducing an interventional radiology treatment alternative in the management of sialorrhea." *Pediatr Radiol.* 2020. DOI: 10.1007/s00247-020-04649-6. PMID: 32198665. — VERIFIED (multi-source agreement: Springer listing, PubMed listing, PMC listing PMC7217813, all concordant on authors/journal/year/DOI/PMID). Technique: image-guided sotradecol (sodium tetradecyl sulfate) + ethanol dual-drug injection; submandibular/sublingual glands injected percutaneously under US guidance; parotid glands injected retrograde via Stensen duct under fluoroscopy (not purely US-guided for the parotid component). Note for the review: this is a distinct clinical indication (pediatric neurologic sialorrhea) from oncologic/tumor ablation and should be kept in a separate sub-section if included at all — it is glandular functional ablation, not lesion-targeted tumor ablation.

### 8c. Experimental / not identified in the literature at all

**Laser ablation of salivary gland tumors specifically:** **No dedicated study of percutaneous laser ablation applied to a salivary gland tumor was found.** Laser ablation literature retrieved was for thyroid (papillary microcarcinoma) and parathyroid adenoma, not salivary gland. The "two-center thermal ablation" paper (Section 8b) covered MWA and RFA only, not laser. **This should be reported in the review as an explicit gap — laser ablation appears essentially unstudied for salivary gland lesions in the indexed literature retrievable via this search session**, in contrast to RFA and MWA which have at least small case series/single trials.

**HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound) for salivary gland tumors:** **No study — case report, series, or trial — applying HIFU to a salivary gland tumor (benign or malignant) was found**, despite multiple targeted search attempts (including searches combining HIFU with parotid, Warthin tumor, pleomorphic adenoma, and sialadenitis). HIFU literature retrieved was for breast, liver, kidney, pancreas, bone, prostate, and parathyroid — not salivary gland. **This is a clear, explicit, and fairly confident gap: HIFU for salivary gland lesions does not appear to be represented in the indexed literature accessible to this search session** and should be described in the review as an unstudied/theoretical modality for this organ, not as "experimental with case reports" (there do not appear to even be case reports).
