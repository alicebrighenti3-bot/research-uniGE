# Elastography of the Salivary Glands — Literature Compilation

Domain: strain elastography (SE) and shear-wave elastography (SWE, 2D-SWE, pSWE) of major
salivary glands — normal gland, sialadenitis/Sjögren, fibrosis, benign vs malignant tumors.

network-blocked (EGRESS_BLOCKED) for every target tried — pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov,

## 2. Key diagnostic studies — benign vs malignant salivary gland tumors (SWE / RTE)

- **Bhatia KS, Cho CC, Tong CS, Lee YY, Yuen EH, Ahuja AT. "Shear wave elastography of
  focal salivary gland lesions: preliminary experience in a routine head and neck US
  clinic." Eur Radiol. 2012;22:957–965.** DOI 10.1007/s00330-011-2364-3. PMID not
  retrieved (search-budget exhausted before confirming). VERIFIED (multi-source — full
  author list, journal, volume/pages recur consistently across independent searches).
  - n=60 lesions (49 parotid, 11 submandibular); 55 benign (21 pleomorphic adenoma [PA],
    18 Warthin tumor [WT], 16 other) + 5 malignant (2 mucoepidermoid carcinoma, 1
    myoepithelial carcinoma, 1 B-cell lymphoma, 1 nodal metastasis).
  - Shear modulus: benign median **18.3 kPa**, malignant median **13.5 kPa** — reported as
    "overlapping appreciably" (i.e., malignant were NOT uniformly stiffer than benign).
  - PA median **22.5 kPa** vs WT **16.9 kPa**.
  - Two mucoepidermoid carcinomas were outliers at the very high end: **81.9 kPa and 132.0
    kPa**.
  - Explicit stated mechanism: PA stiffness can be very high because of its myxochondroid
    stromal component, overlapping malignant tumor stiffness — cited explicitly as the
    reason SWE alone cannot cleanly separate benign from malignant. VERIFIED (this
    explanatory sentence recurred near-verbatim across two independent search syntheses).

## 5. Systematic reviews / meta-analyses

### Tumor (benign vs malignant) elastosonography meta-analyses

- **Wu J, Zhou Z, Wang X, Jin Y, Wang Z, Jin G. "Diagnostic performance of elastosonography
  in the differential diagnosis of benign and malignant salivary gland tumors: A
  meta-analysis." Front Oncol. 2022;12:954751.** DOI 10.3389/fonc.2022.954751; PMCID
  PMC9533713. VERIFIED (multi-source, consistent across 3 independent searches — full
  author list, journal, volume, article number, DOI, PMCID all recovered).
  - Search through PubMed/EMBASE/Cochrane Library, inception to Nov 2021.
  - **16 studies, 1105 patients, 1146 lesions.**
  - Pooled **sensitivity 0.73 (95% CI 0.66–0.78)**, **specificity 0.64 (95% CI
    0.61–0.67)**, **AUC 0.82**.
  - Conclusion (per snippet): quantitative and semiquantitative elastosonography methods
    outperformed qualitative scoring; sonoelastographic measurement technique was the
    largest driver of between-study heterogeneity.

- **"Comparative analysis of elastosonography and 18F-FDG PET/CT in differentiating
  benign and malignant salivary gland tumors: a systematic review and meta-analysis."
  BMC Oral Health. 2025.** PMID 40170169. DOI 10.1186/s12903-025-05809-6. VERIFIED
  (multi-source, consistent across 2 independent searches with matching numbers).
  - **26 studies, 1609 patients.**
  - Elastosonography pooled **sensitivity 0.75 (95% CI 0.65–0.84)**, **specificity 0.79
    (95% CI 0.69–0.87)**, **AUC 0.83**.
  - 18F-FDG PET/CT pooled **sensitivity 0.84 (95% CI 0.78–0.90)**, **specificity 0.85
    (95% CI 0.69–0.96)**, **AUC 0.92**.
  - AUC difference statistically significant, Z=2.77, P<0.01 — PET/CT outperformed
    elastosonography.

  **DISCORDANCE BETWEEN THE TWO TUMOR META-ANALYSES (same evidence base, different
  results, 3 years apart):** the 2022 Front Oncol meta-analysis (16 studies) found
  pooled specificity **0.64**, while the 2025 BMC Oral Health meta-analysis (26 studies,
  presumably including newer/different study sets) found pooled specificity **0.79**
  for what is nominally the same clinical question (benign vs malignant salivary tumor
  differentiation by elastosonography). This ~15-point specificity gap across
  meta-analyses is itself evidence of substantial between-study heterogeneity in the
  underlying primary literature (see Section 6).

- A third, separate reference recurring in searches: "a meta-analysis included **nine
  studies of real-time elastography (RTE)**... pooled sensitivity and specificity were
  **76%**" for benign/malignant differentiation. Could NOT identify the underlying
  citation (author/journal/year) despite repeated targeted searches — this figure may be
  a paraphrase embedded inside one of the narrative reviews above (e.g. summarizing an
  earlier RTE-only meta-analysis distinct from the two elastosonography meta-analyses
  above) rather than a standalone paper. UNVERIFIED-SINGLE-SOURCE / unattributable —
  **do not cite without locating the primary source.**

### Sjögren's syndrome elastography meta-analyses

- **"The diagnostic performance of salivary gland ultrasound elastography in Sjögren's
  syndrome and sicca symptoms: a systematic review and meta-analysis." Eur Radiol. 2023
  Sep.** PMID 37658892. DOI 10.1007/s00330-023-10166-5. VERIFIED (multi-source,

  Aug.** PMCID PMC11331836. VERIFIED citation (multi-source). Search of
  PubMed/MEDLINE/EMBASE/Scopus/Cochrane/CNKI, 1999 to 26 Sept 2022. "Two-dimensional
  transverse wave elastography" here appears to be a synonym/translation for 2D-SWE.
  **Pooled sensitivity/specificity figures NOT retrieved** — GAP.

- **"Does ultrasound elastography have a role as a diagnostic method for Sjögren's

consensus estimate.

## 6. Discordance analysis — why cut-offs differ across studies

Explicit evidence assembled above supporting heterogeneity/non-universality of cut-offs:

1. **Biological confound (tumor histology):** Bhatia 2012 (Eur Radiol) explicitly reports
   that PA stiffness (median 22.5 kPa) can exceed that of some malignant lesions (median
   13.5 kPa) because of its myxochondroid stromal component — meaning a single
   universal kPa threshold cannot cleanly separate benign from malignant salivary tumors
   even in principle, independent of measurement technique.
2. **Wide inter-study performance spread for the same clinical question:** pooled
   specificity for elastosonography differentiating benign/malignant tumors ranged from
   **0.64** (Front Oncol 2022 meta-analysis, 16 studies) to **0.79** (BMC Oral Health
   2025 meta-analysis, 26 studies) — a large discrepancy for nominally the same target
   condition.
3. **Technique/vendor heterogeneity flagged directly by meta-analysts:** the Front Oncol
   2022 meta-analysis explicitly concludes that "sonoelastographic measurement technique"
   (i.e., qualitative RTE score vs semiquantitative strain ratio vs quantitative SWE
   kPa/m-s) was the most significant driver of between-study heterogeneity, and that
   quantitative/semiquantitative methods outperformed qualitative ones.
4. **Meta-regression evidence (Sjögren domain):** the Eur Radiol 2023 meta-analysis
   formally tested — via meta-regression/subgroup analysis — country, diagnostic
   criteria used, imaging system/vendor, specific SWE technique, measurement location
   (parotid vs submandibular; which ROI within gland), control-group definition, and
   patient age as heterogeneity sources, implying all of these were considered plausible
   contributors to inconsistent results across primary studies.
5. **General (non-salivary-specific) technical variability literature:** independent
   phantom/technical studies on shear-wave speed and dispersion quantification identify
   vendor-specific SWE implementation, acquisition depth, and lateral ROI position as
   material sources of measurement variance — plausibly transferable to the salivary
   gland literature, though this specific evidence (ScienceDirect phantom study) was not
   salivary-gland-specific and only its title/topic was retrieved (GAP, not
   independently verified against a salivary-specific validation).
6. **No harmonized reference range for normal tissue:** no single, cross-validated
   normal reference range (kPa or m/s) for the normal parotid/submandibular parenchyma
   was retrieved in this search that is used consistently across the diagnostic-tumor
   and Sjögren-syndrome literatures — each paper appears to define its own local control
   group and its own normal-vs-abnormal threshold. This is consistent with there being
   no accepted universal cut-off. (ScienceDirect: "Influencing Factors, Reference Range
   and Repeatability of Salivary Gland Ultrasound Viscoelastic Imaging in Healthy Adults"
   — title only located, content NOT retrieved — likely directly relevant but unconfirmed,
   GAP.)

**Bottom line on cut-offs:** the assembled evidence does NOT support a universal,
vendor-independent numeric elastography cut-off (kPa or m/s) for either (a) benign vs
malignant salivary tumor differentiation or (b) normal vs Sjögren-affected gland. Pooled
meta-analytic sensitivity/specificity are moderate-to-good but disagree between
meta-analyses of the same question, and the leading tumor-elastography meta-analysis
explicitly attributes this to technique heterogeneity.
