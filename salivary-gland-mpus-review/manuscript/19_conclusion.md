## 19. Conclusion

Returning to this review's central question — how conventional and advanced
multiparametric ultrasound can be integrated to improve diagnosis, characterization,
tissue sampling, and management of salivary gland disease, and which components of this
approach are sufficiently validated for current practice — the evidence assembled here
supports a differentiated, technique-specific answer rather than a single verdict on
"multiparametric ultrasound" as a whole.

B-mode and Doppler ultrasound remain the validated foundation of salivary gland
imaging, with sialolithiasis diagnosis and OMERACT grey-scale scoring in Sjögren's
syndrome standing out as the two most rigorously evidenced applications in this entire
review. Microvascular imaging, elastography, and CEUS each add genuine, technique-
specific diagnostic value — most convincingly and convergently for distinguishing
pleomorphic adenoma from Warthin tumor — without yet supporting a universal cut-off for
the broader and more clinically urgent task of excluding malignancy, for reasons that
are partly biological (pleomorphic adenoma's variable stiffness) and partly a matter of
unresolved technique and vendor heterogeneity that this review has analyzed explicitly
rather than smoothed over. Core needle biopsy has more consistent evidence of superior
sensitivity than fine-needle aspiration for malignancy detection, a finding with direct,
actionable implications for how sampling decisions are sequenced in practice. Genuinely
algorithmic multiparametric data fusion, AI-based classification beyond single-center
proof-of-concept, and most ablative techniques beyond radiofrequency ablation remain,
honestly stated, research directions rather than validated clinical tools.

The diagnostic workflow proposed in Section 16 is offered as a structured way to apply
this differentiated evidence base at the point of care, explicitly labeled as the
authors' clinical proposal rather than a validated algorithm. Its central, recurring
message — that a reassuring ultrasound examination, however multiparametric,
reduces but does not eliminate the probability of malignancy — should guide how
confidently any salivary gland ultrasound report is worded, and when the threshold for
tissue sampling or cross-sectional imaging should be crossed. Advancing this field
further will depend less on additional single-center descriptive studies and more on
prospective, multicenter validation of the multiparametric and AI-assisted approaches
that current evidence identifies as promising but not yet proven.
