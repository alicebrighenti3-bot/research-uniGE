## 17. Limitations and unmet needs

**Operator and interobserver factors.** Every technique reviewed in this manuscript —
grayscale interpretation, Doppler settings, strain elastography compression technique,
SWE ROI placement, CEUS timing and bolus technique, and needle-guidance skill for
tissue sampling — is operator-dependent to a degree not fully captured by pooled
diagnostic-accuracy figures derived largely from single, often high-volume, academic
centers. Reproducibility data specific to this organ are strongest for the OMERACT
grey-scale Sjögren's score (Section 10.1), where the training-dependence of reliability
is explicitly documented, and weakest or entirely absent for CEUS and microvascular
imaging, where no dedicated inter-observer or inter-vendor reproducibility study was
identified in this review.

**Lack of standardization.** No universally accepted normal size range exists for any
major salivary gland (Section 2.6); no dedicated society position statement standardizes
Doppler acquisition parameters for this organ (Section 4.1); elastography cut-offs vary
by vendor, ROI, and technique without a harmonized reference range for normal
parenchyma (Section 6); and quantitative CEUS relies on specific third-party perfusion-
analysis software with untested cross-platform comparability for this organ (Section
7.5).

**Vendor and platform dependence.** The microvascular-imaging literature reviewed here
is drawn almost entirely from a single proprietary technology (SMI); no cross-vendor
comparative study for this organ was identified, so it is not established whether
findings generalize to other manufacturers' slow-flow Doppler implementations. Shear-
wave elastography values are well documented elsewhere in the ultrasound literature to
vary by vendor and acquisition method, and the salivary-gland-specific evidence
reviewed here (Section 6.3) is consistent with, though does not independently establish
the mechanism for, this general principle.

**Small sample sizes and retrospective, single-center designs.** With the exception of
the sialolithiasis, FNA-versus-CNB, and Sjögren's-syndrome elastography meta-analyses,
the great majority of studies underlying this review are single-center, retrospective,
and modestly sized (commonly fewer than 100–150 patients), a limitation that applies
with particular force to the microvascular-imaging, CEUS-malignancy-detection, and
ablation-technique literature.

**Selection, spectrum, and reference-standard bias.** Diagnostic-accuracy studies in
this field are predominantly drawn from patients already referred for tissue sampling
or surgery, enriching the study population for clinically significant disease relative
to a general screening population, and reference standards vary across studies (surgical
histopathology, cytology, sialendoscopy, or clinical/serological composite criteria for
autoimmune disease) in ways that are not always fully comparable — a source of
heterogeneity distinct from, but compounding, the technique-heterogeneity discussed
throughout Sections 4–7.

**Histologic heterogeneity and rarity of malignant tumors.** Malignant salivary gland
tumors comprise numerous distinct histotypes, individually uncommon, which limits the
statistical power of any single-center study to characterize histotype-specific
sonographic features — the likely explanation for the near-total absence of dedicated
sonomorphology literature for individual malignant histotypes documented in Section
12.1, rather than a simple failure to study the question.

**Absence of multicenter, prospective validation.** With few exceptions (the
sialolithiasis meta-analysis, the FNA-versus-CNB meta-analyses, the Sjögren's-syndrome
elastography meta-analyses), this review identified essentially no multicenter,
prospectively validated diagnostic-accuracy study for any advanced mpUS technique
addressed here — a limitation that should be weighed heavily by readers considering
adoption of any specific numeric cut-off presented in the primary literature discussed
in Sections 5–7.

**Absence of universally accepted cut-offs.** As detailed at length in Sections 4 and 6,
neither spectral Doppler nor elastography currently supports a single, vendor-
independent numeric threshold for benign-versus-malignant tumor discrimination — a
conclusion this review reaches not merely from absence of consensus but from documented,
mechanistically explained biological and technical heterogeneity that a larger sample
size alone would not resolve.

**Risk of overfitting in proposed multiparametric models.** Because no study identified
here formally validated a pre-specified, algorithmic combination of all mpUS domains
(Section 8), any multiparametric scoring model reported in the primary literature to
date should be regarded as carrying meaningful risk of overfitting to its derivation
cohort until independently, prospectively validated in a separate population — a caution
that applies equally to the emerging AI/radiomics models discussed in Section 18.

**A note on this review's own evidentiary limitations.** As stated in Section 0 of the
accompanying evidence map and repeated here for transparency: the literature synthesis
underlying this manuscript was compiled without the ability to directly open and read
primary-source full texts or abstracts in the environment used to prepare it network
access to PubMed, Crossref, and publisher sites was unavailable throughout preparation.
Every reference cited here was cross-checked across multiple independent search results
for bibliographic consistency, but individual numeric findings should be independently
re-verified by the submitting authors against the original publications before this
manuscript is finalized for submission.
