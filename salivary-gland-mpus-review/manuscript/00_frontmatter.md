# Multiparametric Ultrasound of the Salivary Glands: From Normal Sonoanatomy to Integrated Diagnostic Pathways for Inflammatory, Autoimmune, and Neoplastic Disease

**Running title:** Multiparametric US of the Salivary Glands

## Abstract (structured, ~300 words — draft)

**Background.** Ultrasound is the first-line imaging modality for the superficial,
high-frequency-accessible major salivary glands, but conventional grayscale and Doppler
assessment has recognized limits in tissue characterization. Multiparametric ultrasound
(mpUS) — the combined, structured use of B-mode, color/power Doppler, microvascular
imaging, elastography, and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) — has been proposed to
close this gap.

**Purpose.** To critically review normal salivary gland sonoanatomy and the evidence
base for mpUS across inflammatory, obstructive, autoimmune, benign neoplastic, and
malignant neoplastic disease, and to define which components of an integrated mpUS
approach are validated for current practice versus promising or experimental.

**Methods.** Narrative review with structured evidence appraisal, prioritizing
systematic reviews, meta-analyses, consensus/guideline statements, and comparative
diagnostic-accuracy studies identified through literature search current to 2026.
Evidence is explicitly graded as consolidated, promising-but-unvalidated, or
experimental/emerging, and discordant findings between studies are analyzed for their
likely technical or population-based origin rather than resolved by selective citation.

**Results.** B-mode plus Doppler ultrasound remains validated for first-line triage and
for sialolithiasis, where diagnostic accuracy is high and consistently reproduced.
Grey-scale ultrasound scoring (OMERACT system) is the most rigorously validated advanced
application, for Sjögren's syndrome, under trained/structured reading conditions.
Elastography and microvascular imaging add diagnostically relevant information for
benign tumor subtyping but do not support a universal, vendor-independent numeric
cut-off; meta-analyses addressing the same clinical question report discordant pooled
accuracy, attributable chiefly to technique and vendor heterogeneity. CEUS is well
supported for differentiating pleomorphic adenoma from Warthin tumor but lacks a pooled
accuracy estimate for malignancy detection. Core needle biopsy shows consistently higher
sensitivity than fine-needle aspiration for malignancy across independent comparative
studies. Genuinely algorithmic multiparametric data fusion, US–MRI fusion, and most
thermal ablation techniques beyond radiofrequency ablation remain experimental or
unstudied for this organ.

**Conclusion.** mpUS should be integrated into salivary gland diagnostic pathways as a
structured, technique-by-technique reasoning process rather than a single validated
test; this review proposes such a workflow while explicitly flagging its unvalidated,
expert-opinion status.

**Keywords:** salivary glands; ultrasonography; elastography; contrast-enhanced
ultrasound; microvascular imaging; Sjögren syndrome; fine-needle aspiration; core needle
biopsy
