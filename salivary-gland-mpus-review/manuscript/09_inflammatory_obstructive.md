## 9. Inflammatory and obstructive diseases

### 9.1 Acute, chronic, and recurrent sialadenitis

Ultrasound is an established first-line tool for inflammatory and obstructive salivary
gland disease, reported useful in the large majority of presenting cases in at least one
structured review of the literature combined with institutional experience, though the
authors of that review note that robust comparative-accuracy data are considerably more
developed for obstructive disease (sialolithiasis, tumors) than for pure, non-obstructive
inflammatory disease (Koch et al., *J Clin Med* 2021). Acute sialadenitis typically shows
glandular enlargement with hypoechoic, heterogeneous parenchyma, hypo- or anechoic foci
where microabscess formation is present, ductal dilation in bacterial/purulent forms, and
increased color/power Doppler flow (hyperemia). Chronic sialadenitis instead shows
heterogeneous echogenicity reflecting parenchymal fibrosis, ductal dilation, and
glandular atrophy, typically with reduced rather than increased vascularity. Juvenile
recurrent parotitis produces a distinctive scattered pattern of multiple small
hypoechoic foci — commonly described as a "Swiss cheese" or "moth-eaten" appearance —
with hypervascularity during acute episodes and reduced vascularity between episodes;
sialendoscopy has an established therapeutic as well as diagnostic role in this
condition. Advanced techniques (elastography, CEUS) have not, on the evidence
identified in this review, been systematically studied for non-obstructive sialadenitis
specifically, in contrast to their more developed evidence base in Sjögren's syndrome
(Section 10) — a gap worth stating explicitly.

### 9.2 Sialolithiasis

Ultrasound diagnosis of sialolithiasis is one of the best-evidenced diagnostic
applications in this entire review. A 2022 meta-analysis of 10 studies (1,393 patients)
using sialendoscopy, sialography, or surgery as reference standards reported a pooled
diagnostic odds ratio of 162.6 and a summary receiver-operating-characteristic AUC of
0.96, with parotid-gland calculi detected somewhat more accurately than submandibular
calculi in subgroup analysis (Kim et al., *Laryngoscope* 2022). Individual comparative
studies against direct stone identification or surgical reference standards report
sensitivities in the 77–95% range and specificities in the 95–97% range (Terraz et al.,
*AJR* 2013; Goncalves et al., *J Ultrasound Med* 2017), though at least one more recent
comparative study reported internally inconsistent sensitivity figures for ultrasound
within its own published synthesis (65% versus 86%, depending on which part of the
study's results are cited), a discrepancy that should be resolved against the primary
text rather than silently picked before being used in any future citation. Classic
sonographic findings are an echogenic focus with posterior acoustic shadowing and
proximal ductal dilation; small or purely intraductal stones, and stones located deep
within the distal Wharton's duct obscured by mandibular shadowing, remain recognized
sources of false-negative examination.

### 9.3 Ductal stenosis

Ultrasound can identify pre-stenotic ductal dilation and localize the general
transition point of a suspected stricture, but a normal-caliber duct on ultrasound does
not exclude stenosis, and sialendoscopy remains the reference standard for definitive
diagnosis, characterization, and — commonly in the same procedure — therapeutic
management of ductal strictures, with gland preservation achieved in the majority of
appropriately selected sialendoscopy cases per the general sialendoscopy literature.
Dedicated, high-quality primary literature specifically on the diagnostic accuracy of
ultrasound for ductal stenosis (as distinct from sialolithiasis) is comparatively
sparse and largely descriptive rather than accuracy-focused — an explicit gap.

### 9.4 Abscess

Salivary gland abscess typically appears as a heterogeneous fluid collection with an
anechoic or hypoechoic center and an isoechoic-to-hyperechoic inflammatory rim, with
increased peripheral vascularity on Doppler, in contrast to the homogeneous, mildly
hyperechoic appearance of normal parenchyma. Ultrasound-guided needle aspiration is
reported as a safe, effective, less invasive alternative to surgical incision and
drainage for parotid abscess in at least one comparative study, albeit with a higher
average number of repeat procedures required (approximately 1.9 aspirations versus 1.1
surgical interventions per patient in that series) — a trade-off between invasiveness
and procedural efficiency that should be discussed with the patient rather than
presented as a straightforwardly superior option in every case (see also Section 14.6).

### 9.5 Summary appraisal

Ultrasound diagnosis of sialolithiasis is **consolidated, high-accuracy evidence** —
among the strongest diagnostic claims in this review. Grayscale-and-Doppler diagnosis
of sialadenitis in its various forms is **well established descriptively** but rests on
a comparatively thinner formal-accuracy evidence base, and ductal stenosis assessment by
ultrasound alone is **an adjunct, not a substitute, for sialendoscopy**. Advanced
multiparametric techniques remain largely unstudied for this disease category outside
the Sjögren's-syndrome-specific literature reviewed in Section 10.
