## Figure legends

**Figure 1. Normal sonoanatomy of the parotid, submandibular, and sublingual glands.**
(A) Transverse parotid scan showing the retromandibular vein as a surrogate landmark
for the facial nerve plane, separating the notional superficial and deep lobes. (B)
Longitudinal parotid scan showing the gland's relationship to the mandibular ramus and
masseter muscle. (C) Submandibular gland with adjacent facial artery and vein at the
mandibular angle, and the mylohyoid muscle. (D) Sublingual gland on floor-of-mouth
transverse scan, between mylohyoid and genioglossus. (E) Schematic diagram of Stensen's
and Wharton's duct anatomy, annotated to emphasize that non-visualization — not a
measured calibre — is the expected normal finding. *Message: establishes the normal
anatomic and vascular-landmark reference frame against which every subsequent pathology
figure in the manuscript should be read, and makes explicit the acoustic-shadowing
limitation (deep parotid lobe) referenced throughout the text.*

**Figure 2. Multiparametric ultrasound workflow (schematic).**
Flow diagram: clinical question → B-mode → color/power Doppler → microvascular imaging
(where available) → elastography → CEUS (selected cases) → tissue sampling (FNA/CNB) →
CT/MRI (selected cases) → management decision. Each arrow/step is annotated with its
evidence grade (validated / promising / experimental) drawn directly from Table 2 and
Table 8. A boxed note states explicitly: *"Author-proposed clinical pathway — not a
validated algorithm or society guideline (see Section 8 and Section 16)."*
*Message: operationalizes the review's central evidence-grading argument as a usable
clinical sequence, while keeping its unvalidated status visually explicit rather than
implied.*

**Figure 3. Representative multiparametric assessment of a focal salivary gland
lesion.**
(A) B-mode showing lesion morphology (margin, echotexture, cystic component). (B)
Color/power Doppler showing gross vascular distribution. (C) Microvascular imaging
(SMI) showing finer vessel architecture and branching pattern not resolved on
conventional Doppler. (D) Shear-wave elastography color map with stiffness
measurement (kPa), with a caption note reiterating that stiffness does not, by itself,
indicate malignancy (Section 6.3). (E) CEUS arterial-phase image and corresponding
time-intensity curve, annotated with time-to-peak and wash-in/wash-out pattern.
*Message: shows concretely, on a single lesion, what each technique adds — and does not
resolve — illustrating incremental diagnostic value and residual ambiguity rather than
presenting a clean, technique-driven diagnosis.*

**Figure 4. Diagnostic pathway for focal salivary gland lesions.**
Decision-tree figure distinguishing: (i) lesions where B-mode ± Doppler alone is
sufficient to complete the work-up (e.g., a classic sialolith); (ii) lesions where
advanced techniques (MVI, elastography, CEUS) are reasonably added, chiefly for
benign-tumor subtyping; (iii) lesions where tissue sampling is indicated regardless of
how reassuring the multiparametric findings are; and (iv) findings that mandate MRI/CT
irrespective of ultrasound appearance (deep-lobe/parapharyngeal extension, suspected
perineural spread, staging of confirmed malignancy). *Message: operationalizes the
workflow of Figure 2 into concrete triage decisions and gives visual weight to the
review's recurring caution that a reassuring scan does not exclude malignancy.*

**Figure 5. Ultrasound-guided FNA/CNB procedural approach.**
(A) Diagram of needle trajectory using a posterior-inferior approach from the
superficial parotid tail, with a shallow angle to the skin to avoid the parotid duct and
the facial nerve plane (retromandibular-vein landmark from Figure 1). (B) B-mode image
showing needle targeting of a parotid lesion with the intended safe approach angle
annotated. (C) Color Doppler "roadmap" of vessels to be avoided during needle placement.
*Message: grounds practical procedural guidance directly in the anatomy established in
Figure 1, reinforcing that safe intervention depends on the same landmark reasoning used
for diagnostic scanning.*
