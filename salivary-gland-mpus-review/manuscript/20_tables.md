### Table 1. Normal ultrasound anatomy and technical examination of major salivary glands

| Gland | Location / boundaries | Key landmarks | Duct | Normal B-mode appearance | Technique notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parotid | Preauricular/retromandibular region; superficial to masseter and mandibular ramus, deep to styloid process and its musculature | Retromandibular vein (surrogate for facial nerve plane, not directly visualized); external carotid artery branches | Stensen's duct — not visualized unless dilated; sialagogue provocation can aid visualization | Homogeneously hyperechoic relative to muscle (fat content); fine, uniform echotexture | High-frequency linear probe (~5–14 MHz per various sources, not fully standardized); low-frequency curved probe adjunct for deep lobe; deep lobe/parapharyngeal extension not reliably assessed (mandibular shadowing) |
| Submandibular | Submandibular triangle; anterior/posterior digastric bellies, inferior mandible border; wraps around posterior mylohyoid free edge | Facial artery/vein at mandibular angle | Wharton's duct — not visualized unless dilated | Slightly less echogenic, coarser than parotid; hyperechoic relative to muscle | Standardized technique proposal exists (Hoffman et al. 2024, AAO-HNS) — submandibular only; no equivalent parotid-specific standard identified |
| Sublingual | Floor of mouth; between mylohyoid (inferolateral) and genioglossus (medial), superior to mylohyoid sling | Adjacent lingual vessels | Numerous small excretory ducts (~8–20), independent or joining Wharton's duct | Small, deep; less consistently fully visualized | Complete depiction inherently operator/patient-dependent given size and depth |
| Minor salivary glands | Oral mucosa, lips, palate | — | Multiple small ducts | Only inconsistently accessible transcutaneously | Assessed mainly in disease-specific context (e.g., sicca-syndrome US vs. labial biopsy comparison), not as a routine survey target |
| Lymph nodes (intra-/periglandular) | Parotid tail, along retromandibular vein; periglandular cervical chains | — | — | Small, oval, preserved echogenic hilum (normal) | Included in a complete examination given relevance to inflammatory and neoplastic disease |

*No universally accepted normal size range exists for any major salivary gland; age,
body mass index, and metabolic status are documented confounds on normal echogenicity
and homogeneity (Section 2.6).*

### Table 2. Multiparametric ultrasound techniques: principle, parameter assessed, advantages, limitations, and current clinical role

| Technique | Principle | Parameter(s) assessed | Advantages | Limitations | Current clinical role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-mode | Grayscale tissue reflectivity | Size, echogenicity, margin, shape, cystic/necrotic change, calcification, ductal calibre | Ubiquitous, fast, no contrast, high specificity for many findings | Moderate sensitivity for malignancy; overlapping benign/malignant appearance | **Validated** — first-line, foundational |
| Color/power Doppler | Doppler shift from moving blood | Presence/pattern of macrovascular flow | Simple, fast, widely available | Low sensitivity to slow flow; discordant/unpooled spectral cut-offs for tumor characterization | **Validated for inflammatory hyperemia; promising but unvalidated for tumor spectral cut-offs** |
| Microvascular imaging (SMI/MVI) | Adaptive clutter suppression for slow-flow detection | Vessel density, distribution, branching pattern | More sensitive to slow flow than conventional Doppler; no contrast needed | Vendor-specific (SMI-dominated evidence); no completed systematic review; possible duplicate-publication issue in key studies | **Promising, not yet validated** — useful adjunct for PA-vs-Warthin subtyping |
| Strain elastography | Tissue deformation under compression | Strain ratio, elasticity score | No specialized hardware beyond standard probe | Operator-dependent compression; depth-sensitive | **Promising, not yet validated** |
| Shear-wave elastography (2D-SWE) | Shear-wave propagation velocity | Velocity (m/s) / stiffness (kPa) | More operator-independent than strain elastography | No universal cut-off; PA stiffness overlaps/exceeds malignant tumor stiffness (biological confound); vendor/ROI heterogeneity | **Validated (pooled) for Sjögren's syndrome adjunct; promising but not validated for benign-vs-malignant tumor differentiation** |
| CEUS | Microbubble contrast perfusion kinetics | Enhancement pattern, TIC parameters (TTP, nMTT, AUC, wash-in/out) | Real-time perfusion information; no nephrotoxicity | No dedicated society guideline confirmed; no pooled malignancy-detection accuracy; software/vendor-dependent quantification | **Validated for PA-vs-Warthin differentiation (pooled meta-analysis); promising but not validated for malignancy detection** |
| Algorithmic multiparametric fusion (AI-based) | Machine-learning combination of multiple US-derived feature sets | Composite malignancy/histology probability | Potential to integrate all domains objectively | **Not identified as formally studied for this organ** | **Experimental/not yet studied** |

### Table 6. Differential diagnosis by pattern: B-mode vs Doppler vs microvascular imaging vs elastography vs CEUS

| Pattern | Leading differential | Most informative technique(s) | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffuse homogeneous enlargement | Early sialadenitis; early Sjögren's; sialosis; normal variant | B-mode + clinical/serologic context | Grayscale alone insufficient |
| Diffuse heterogeneous enlargement | Chronic/recurrent sialadenitis; Sjögren's (grade 2–3); IgG4-RD; sarcoidosis; diffuse lymphoma/metastasis | OMERACT B-mode score, Doppler, elastography | Requires serology/clinical correlation |
| Focal well-defined hypoechoic lesion | PA, Warthin, oncocytoma, benign node | B-mode + MVI/elastography/CEUS for PA-vs-Warthin | Well-defined margin reduces but does not exclude malignancy |
| Focal ill-defined/infiltrative lesion | Malignant tumor; aggressive benign process; abscess | B-mode; tissue sampling | Higher pretest probability for sampling |
| Hypervascular lesion | Warthin tumor; inflammatory/infected focus; some malignant tumors | MVI more informative than conventional Doppler | Vascularity alone does not separate benign/malignant |
| Hypovascular/avascular lesion | PA; simple cyst; some early-stage malignant tumors | MVI; caution advised | Hypovascularity is not reassuring in isolation |
| Cystic lesion | Warthin; simple cyst; lymphoepithelial cyst; necrotic malignant tumor | B-mode wall/mural-nodule assessment | Thick/irregular wall raises concern |
| Calcified lesion | Sialolith (best-evidenced pattern); nonspecific tumor calcification | B-mode + ductal dilation | Sialolith pattern well-validated; tumor calcification is not specific |
| Stiff lesion (SWE) | PA (often) as much as malignant tumors | Elastography, but with explicit caution | Do not equate stiffness with malignancy |
| Marked SMI/MVI vascularity | Warthin most consistently | MVI | Not reliably distinguishing from some malignant tumors |
| Rapid CEUS wash-in/wash-out | Malignant tumor more often; PA can mimic | CEUS quantitative TIC | Overlap documented, not deterministic |
| Multifocal/bilateral disease | Warthin (bilateral); Sjögren's; IgG4-RD; sarcoidosis; lymphoma; HIV-related disease | Pattern + serology | Bilateral metastatic nodal disease is comparatively uncommon |

### Table 7. FNA versus core needle biopsy: indications, diagnostic performance, advantages, disadvantages, complications

| Feature | US-guided FNA | US-guided core needle biopsy (CNB) |
|---|---|---|
| Reported sensitivity for malignancy | ~68% (largest recent meta-analysis, 2025/2026) | ~99% (same meta-analysis) |
| Non-diagnostic / inconclusive rate | Higher (19% in one direct comparison) | Lower (4% in the same comparison) |
| Reporting framework | Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (6-tier, 2nd edition 2023/2024) | No equivalently codified international histopathology-reporting system identified |
| Invasiveness | Lower (fine needle, 23–27G typical) | Higher (18–20G core needle, automated device) |
| Complications | Minimal; hematoma most common | Minimal; hematoma most common; no confirmed permanent facial nerve injury or tumor seeding identified in the literature reviewed |
| Speed / availability | Faster, more widely available | Requires specific equipment/expertise |
| Rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE) | Plausible adjunct; **no salivary-gland-specific outcome study identified** | Not typically applicable in the same way |
| Direction of comparative evidence | Consistent across ≥3 independent studies (Eom 2015; Cho 2020; Kassem 2025/2026): CNB superior for malignancy sensitivity and non-diagnostic rate | |
