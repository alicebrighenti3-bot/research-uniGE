## 5. Microvascular imaging

### 5.1 Principle

Microvascular imaging techniques — marketed under vendor-specific names including
Superb Microvascular Imaging (SMI, Canon/Toshiba), MicroFlow Imaging or MV-Flow
(Samsung), and comparable proprietary implementations from other manufacturers — use
adaptive clutter-suppression algorithms to separate genuine low-velocity blood flow
signal from tissue motion artifact without the wall-filter losses that limit
conventional color and power Doppler at low flow velocities. The result is
substantially greater sensitivity to slow flow in small-caliber vessels, without the
need for contrast agents. It is important not to conflate these as a single generic
technology: they are distinct proprietary implementations with different underlying
clutter-suppression algorithms, and cross-vendor comparative data for salivary gland
applications were not identified in this review — essentially all of the salivary-gland
microvascular-imaging literature identified here uses SMI specifically, and no
MicroFlow Imaging, MicroV, or other vendor-specific alternative was found applied to
this organ. Findings described below should therefore be understood as SMI-specific
until cross-vendor validation is available, not as generic "microvascular imaging"
findings.

### 5.2 Vascular pattern analysis in benign tumor subtyping

The most consistent application of SMI in this organ is differentiating pleomorphic
adenoma from Warthin tumor, a clinically meaningful distinction because management
differs (enucleation is often feasible for Warthin tumor, while pleomorphic adenoma
typically requires wider excision given its risk of recurrence and rare malignant
transformation). Multiple single- and dual-center studies report a consistent
directional pattern: pleomorphic adenoma tends to show sparse, peripheral, or avascular
distribution on SMI, while Warthin tumor tends to show more central or mixed, higher-
grade vascularity, with vessel density and a semiquantitative vascular-index score both
reported higher in Warthin tumor than pleomorphic adenoma (Ryoo et al., *J Ultrasound
Med* 2018, n = 62). Two additional, frequently cited 2020 studies report closely similar
cohort compositions, vascular-index terminology, and near-identical directional findings
for pleomorphic adenoma, Warthin tumor, and basal cell adenoma; whether these represent
genuinely independent confirmatory datasets or overlapping/duplicate reporting from the
same underlying cohort could not be resolved in this review and should be clarified by
the authors before either is cited as independent corroborating evidence.

### 5.3 Comparison with conventional Doppler

Across the studies identified, SMI is consistently reported as more sensitive than
conventional color or power Doppler flow imaging for depicting vessel number,
distribution, and branching pattern within salivary gland tumors, consistent with its
underlying technical rationale. However, **no completed systematic review or
meta-analysis of SMI diagnostic accuracy for salivary gland tumor characterization was
identified** — the only matching item found in this review's search was an apparently
unfulfilled 2021 review protocol, with no corresponding completed publication located.
Quantitative indices (vessel density, vascular index) vary in definition and threshold
across the available studies, and no cross-study pooled cut-off can currently be
recommended.

### 5.4 Adjacent evidence: lymph nodes and diffuse glandular disease

In cervical lymph nodes (not restricted to salivary-region basins), SMI has been
reported to outperform power Doppler for distinguishing benign from malignant nodes,
based on vessel branching pattern (hilar/parallel in benign nodes versus aberrant,
subcapsular, or dysplastic branching in malignant nodes), though this evidence is not
salivary-gland-specific and should be cited as adjacent, extrapolated evidence rather
than direct data (see Section 13). Within the salivary glands themselves, SMI-based
quantitative parenchymal vascularity indices have also been explored as a potential
adjunct in Sjögren's syndrome, where parenchymal vascularity is reported significantly
higher than in healthy controls — a diffuse-disease application distinct from, and less
mature than, its use in focal tumor characterization (see Section 10).

### 5.5 Summary appraisal

SMI/MVI for benign salivary tumor subtyping (particularly pleomorphic adenoma versus
Warthin tumor) is a **promising, mechanistically well-grounded but not yet formally
pooled or externally validated** application: directionally consistent across the
available single-center studies, technically more sensitive than conventional Doppler
by design, but lacking a completed systematic review, a resolved duplicate-publication
question, and any cross-vendor validation. It should currently be regarded as a useful
adjunct to grayscale and elastographic assessment rather than a stand-alone diagnostic
test, and its role in malignant-tumor subtype characterization specifically remains
essentially unstudied in the literature identified for this review.
