## 11. Benign focal lesions and tumors

### 11.1 Pleomorphic adenoma

The most common salivary gland tumor overall, pleomorphic adenoma typically presents
sonographically as a well-defined, lobulated, homogeneously hypoechoic mass with
posterior acoustic enhancement, predominantly peripheral or sparse (grade 0–1)
vascularity on color Doppler and microvascular imaging, and — the pattern most
distinctive of this tumor among all techniques reviewed — a stiffness on shear-wave
elastography that can be high and can overlap with or exceed that of some malignant
tumors, owing to its myxochondroid stromal component (Section 6.3). On CEUS,
pleomorphic adenoma more often shows homogeneous or "slow-in" enhancement with a
comparatively long time to peak and normalized mean transit time relative to Warthin
tumor (Section 7.3). Cystic change is comparatively uncommon (reported in a small
minority of cases in comparative series) and, when marked, should prompt reconsideration
of the diagnosis rather than being assumed typical.

### 11.2 Warthin tumor

Warthin tumor (papillary cystadenoma lymphomatosum) is the second most common benign
parotid tumor, characteristically multiple or bilateral in a meaningful minority of
cases, and typically shows a more heterogeneous, often partly cystic appearance with
anechoic components reported in a substantial proportion of cases, in contrast to
pleomorphic adenoma. Vascularity is characteristically more central and higher-grade
(grade 2–3) on color Doppler and microvascular imaging than pleomorphic adenoma
(Section 5.2), and CEUS more often shows a "slow-out" pattern with a shorter time to
peak and normalized mean transit time than pleomorphic adenoma (Section 7.3).
Elastographic stiffness is generally, though not invariably, lower than pleomorphic
adenoma. Because enucleation is often oncologically adequate for Warthin tumor while
pleomorphic adenoma typically warrants wider excision, the pleomorphic
adenoma-versus-Warthin tumor distinction is the single most clinically actionable, and
best evidence-supported, benign-tumor discrimination task addressed by mpUS in this
review — supported convergently by microvascular imaging, elastography, and CEUS, even
though each technique individually carries the caveats detailed in Sections 5–7.

### 11.3 Oncocytoma, basal cell adenoma, lipoma, and cysts

Oncocytoma appears sonographically as a hypoechoic, well-defined mass without features
that reliably distinguish it from other benign parotid tumors — a genuine grayscale
overlap problem repeatedly noted in the literature identified. Basal cell adenoma, the
next most common benign parotid tumor after pleomorphic adenoma and Warthin tumor in
most series, may show cystic degeneration, though the primary reports of this finding
in the literature identified were not confirmed as ultrasound-based (imaging modality
was more often CT or MRI in the retrieved sources) and this should not be presented as
established sonographic knowledge without further verification. **No dedicated,
well-characterized primary ultrasound-morphology study for basal cell adenoma, lipoma,
or simple/benign salivary cysts was identified in this review** — a genuine gap that
should be stated plainly rather than filled with extrapolation from general soft-tissue
ultrasound principles (e.g., that lipomas are typically well-defined, mildly hyperechoic
to isoechoic, compressible masses, or that simple cysts are anechoic with posterior
enhancement, both standard general-ultrasound expectations not specifically validated
for this organ in the literature reviewed).

### 11.4 Benign lymphoepithelial lesions

Benign lymphoepithelial cysts and lesions, most classically associated with HIV
infection (Section 10.4), show a wide morphologic spectrum from simple cyst to mixed
solid-cystic mass, with mixed lesions showing greater vascularity and low-resistance
arterial flow than purely cystic lesions in the small body of literature identified;
sonographic appearance is explicitly described as nonspecific, particularly for the
mixed solid-cystic pattern, which can resemble neoplasm.

### 11.5 Summary appraisal

The pleomorphic adenoma-versus-Warthin tumor distinction is **the best-supported
multiparametric application among all benign focal lesions**, convergently informed by
grayscale morphology, microvascular imaging, elastography, and CEUS. Beyond this
specific pair, primary ultrasound-morphology evidence for the remaining common benign
tumor types (oncocytoma, basal cell adenoma, lipoma, cysts) is **sparse or absent**, and
this review explicitly declines to present confident sonographic differentiating
criteria for these entities where the underlying literature does not support them.
