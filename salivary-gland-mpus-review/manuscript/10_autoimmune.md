## 10. Autoimmune and systemic diseases

### 10.1 Sjögren's syndrome: the OMERACT salivary gland ultrasound score

Salivary gland ultrasonography (SGUS) in Sjögren's syndrome is, on the evidence
assembled for this review, the single most rigorously developed and validated advanced
application of salivary gland ultrasound of any disease category considered in this
manuscript, and is treated in correspondingly greater depth here.

The Outcome Measures in Rheumatology (OMERACT) ultrasound working group has, across a
sequence of consensus and reliability publications beginning with a 2017 core-item
consensus definition, developed a semiquantitative 0–3 grey-scale scoring system based
principally on parenchymal inhomogeneity of the parotid and submandibular glands, with
grades 0–1 considered normal and 2–3 pathological (Jousse-Joulin et al., *RMD Open*
2017; *Ann Rheum Dis* 2019). A subsequent OMERACT reliability exercise extended this
work toward incorporating glandular vascularization/inflammation scoring (Jousse-Joulin
et al., *Rheumatology* 2022). Reported diagnostic performance of the 2019 OMERACT score
against 2016 ACR/EULAR classification criteria is good, with sensitivity in the low-70%
range and specificity above 90% in at least one comparative study using a total-score
cut-off in at least one gland (Robin et al., *Arthritis Care Res* 2022); a larger pooled
meta-analytic estimate across multiple scoring systems reports sensitivities in the
75–84% range and specificities in the 88–95% range with a summary AUC of 0.93–0.95
(*Scientific Reports* 2018).

**Reproducibility** deserves explicit, honest treatment rather than a single quoted
figure. OMERACT reliability exercises using structured training conditions (video
clips, workshops) report good to excellent inter- and intra-observer agreement; a
dedicated atlas-based pilot study found that, without a reference atlas, inter- and
intra-reader reliability were low, improving to moderate (mean kappa 0.52, range
0.31–0.77) for inter-reader and good (mean kappa 0.69, range 0.46–0.86) for intra-reader
agreement once a reference atlas was introduced, and a subsequent international web-based
training initiative similarly aimed at improving reliability among sonographers of
varying experience levels. **The honest synthesis is that reliability of the OMERACT
grey-scale score is good-to-excellent for experienced or specifically trained readers
under structured/exercise conditions, and demonstrably improves further with reference
materials or formal training — but is more modest among less experienced readers or in
unstructured real-world practice**, and reliability for individual sub-items
(echogenicity, homogeneity grading) is generally lower than for overall gestalt
scoring. This nuance should be represented plainly rather than citing a single
reliability figure as if it applied universally across all reader experience levels and
practice settings.

**Correlation with disease activity and biopsy** is inconsistent across the literature
identified: reported correlations between SGUS score and the clinical ESSDAI activity
index range from strong in some cohorts to statistically non-significant in others, and
concordance between SGUS score and minor salivary gland biopsy (focus score) has been
reported at roughly two-thirds agreement in at least one single-center cohort, with
sensitivity and specificity of the SGUS score against biopsy in the moderate range in
that same study. This heterogeneity across cohorts should be represented as such rather
than resolved into a single correlation coefficient. Current EULAR-associated management
guidance (per this review's search, not independently confirmed against primary text and
flagged for verification before citation) reportedly frames SGUS as relevant chiefly to
lymphoma-risk stratification in patients with specific risk features (elevated
cryoglobulins, salivary gland mass, persistent lymphadenopathy, unexplained systemic
symptoms, or high ESSDAI), rather than as a routine screening tool for all patients with
suspected Sjögren's syndrome.

**Quantitative techniques beyond grey-scale scoring** — Doppler-based parenchymal
vascularity, SMI-based vascularity indices, and 2D-SWE — have each been explored as
adjuncts, generally showing significantly higher vascularity or stiffness in Sjögren's
syndrome patients than in healthy controls, and combined grey-scale-plus-SWE approaches
have been reported to modestly improve area-under-the-curve performance over grey-scale
scoring alone in at least one study. These remain adjunctive and less validated than the
core grey-scale OMERACT score itself.

### 10.2 IgG4-related disease

IgG4-related sialadenitis (including Mikulicz disease, with bilateral lacrimal and
salivary gland involvement, and Küttner tumor, typically an isolated submandibular
presentation) shows a sonographic pattern of multiple hypoechoic areas of varying size
within symmetrically enlarged glands, with increased vascularity, that has been reported
useful in differentiating this entity from Sjögren's syndrome in at least one dedicated
comparative imaging study (Shimizu et al., *Arthritis Res Ther* 2015). Overlap with
Sjögren-pattern sonographic change (multiple hypoechoic zones, glandular inhomogeneity)
is explicitly acknowledged as a diagnostic challenge in the literature identified, and
the evidence base, while real, is considerably thinner than for Sjögren's syndrome —
limited to a small number of studies, largely single-center.

### 10.3 Sarcoidosis

Salivary gland involvement in sarcoidosis is sonographically nonspecific (hypoechoic
nodules, occasional microcalcification, increased vascularity, overlapping with chronic
sialadenitis and other granulomatous disease) and the evidence base identified for this
review is essentially confined to case reports and general head-and-neck imaging
reviews; **no validated sarcoidosis-specific sonographic scoring system was identified**,
and this should be stated as a genuine evidence gap rather than implied to be
comparably developed to the Sjögren's-syndrome literature.

### 10.4 HIV-related salivary gland disease

HIV-associated salivary gland disease, spanning diffuse infiltrative lymphocytosis
syndrome (DILS) and benign lymphoepithelial cysts/lesions, produces a sonographic
spectrum from diffuse lymphocytic infiltration with multiple small cysts (the classic
pre-antiretroviral-therapy pattern) to solid or mixed cystic-solid nodules, with reported
low-resistance arterial flow in mixed nodules and explicitly nonspecific appearance,
particularly for mixed lesions that can mimic neoplasm. The evidence base is largely
older (1990s–2000s case series) with a shift in presentation pattern reported since the
introduction of combination antiretroviral therapy; **no OMERACT-type validated scoring
system exists for this indication**, and modern, large systematic cohort data in the
post-antiretroviral-therapy era were not identified in this review.

### 10.5 Summary appraisal

The OMERACT grey-scale SGUS score for Sjögren's syndrome is **the most validated
advanced sonographic scoring system addressed in this entire review**, with the
important caveat that its reliability is training- and experience-dependent rather than
uniformly excellent across all readers and settings. IgG4-related disease sonography is
a **real but comparatively thin evidence base**. Sarcoidosis and HIV-related salivary
gland disease sonography remain, on current evidence, **descriptive and case-level**
rather than validated diagnostic applications.
